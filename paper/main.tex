Test-time training (TTT) methods for language models adapt a set of fast weights while reading and discard them at every document boundary. We study what a fast weight carried into the next document does. Building on In-Place TTT in Qwen3-0.6B, we introduce a gated carry, in which the state accumulated over earlier text is read through a separate, zero-initialised gated path and trained by backpropagation across slice boundaries. Compared with resetting the state, the gated carry lowers perplexity on the continuation of a document by about 1\%, whereas carrying the state through the existing TTT path gains at most 0.027 within a document and nothing measurable across documents. Across documents the gated carry's effect depends on how the documents relate. On a preregistered test set of 200 Wikipedia targets, at two training seeds, it helps most after the target's own opening, less after a related article and less again after an unrelated one, and it raises perplexity after text from another source. Most of the gain is a generic effect of the source, but a related article still outperforms a carry averaged over 200 articles. Training across document boundaries removes the harm and, with it, this advantage of related text, at two seeds. The gated carry also beats a control with the same architecture and parameters but no fast-weight updates, and trained to the end of its schedule its transfer becomes more specific to related text. All evidence is perplexity, so the findings concern stream and domain adaptation rather than recall.

\chapter{INTRODUCTION}
\label{ch-intro}

A language model adapts to its input through attention, so whatever it gains from a document is lost once the document leaves its context. Test-time training (TTT) offers a second channel. Part of the model is updated while it reads, and In-Place TTT \citep{feng2026inplace} does this inside the MLP blocks of an existing transformer. Every such method, however, resets its fast weights at the end of each document. This report asks what happens if the reset is removed: whether a fast weight built on one document helps or harms the next, and on what this depends.

We answer in perplexity on held-out text. In the terms of \citet{song2026beyond}, this is evidence of stream and domain adaptation. It does not show that a carried state stores information that the model can later retrieve, which would require behavioural tests, and we make no such claim.

\section{Background of the study}

A trained large language model answers every input with the same parameters. This makes inference efficient, but it also means the model cannot specialise to the context in which it is being used. In-context learning lets a model adapt to its input without changing any weight, but only while that input stays in its context window \citep{brown2020language,dong2024survey}. Test-time training relaxes this restriction by updating part of the model on the input it is currently reading, using a self-supervised objective that needs no labels \citep{sun2020ttt}. Applied to language models, TTT has been used to build sequence layers whose hidden state is itself a small model trained online \citep{sun2025learning}, long-term memory modules placed beside attention \citep{behrouz2025titans}, and In-Place TTT \citep{feng2026inplace}, which turns the MLP down-projection of an existing transformer into a fast weight that adapts while the model reads. These methods descend from fast weights, weights that change during inference and act as an associative memory of what has been read \citep{ba2016using,schlag2021linear}.

\section{Justification of the problem}

Every one of these methods resets its adapted state at the end of each document. In-Place TTT does so deliberately, to prevent information leaking between unrelated inputs, and does not test carrying the fast weights forward \citep{feng2026inplace}. Related work approaches persistence from other directions. Some methods meta-learn a test-time initialisation or update weights over one long context \citep{tandon2025endtoend,bansal2025letsnot,wang2026learning}, and others carry cached activations or memory tokens across segments of a single stream \citep{dai2019transformerxl,rae2019compressive,bulatov2022rmt}. Dynamic evaluation adapts a model along its test sequence, and has been applied to successive texts, but by gradient steps on the slow weights \citep{krause2018dynamic,lazaridou2021mind}, and test-time training on retrieved neighbours of an input helps but is reset after every input \citep{hardt2024test}. Carrying a state across packed documents is not new in itself. Selective state-space models learn to pass or reset their state at document boundaries \citep{gu2024mamba}, and in pre-training, earlier documents in the attention context help when they are related and distract when they are not \citep{zhao2024analysing}. A carried fast weight could therefore accumulate useful adaptation across a stream of related documents, or interfere with the next one. To our knowledge, no work gives a TTT fast weight a trained carry path and measures how its effect on the next document depends on how the two documents relate.

\section{Objectives of the study}

The report addresses two research questions.
\begin{description}
  \item[Q1.] How much does the carry, the information retained from the previous document, contribute to language modelling?
  \item[Q2.] Which domains benefit most from session-persistent TTT?
\end{description}

The objective is to develop and analyse a session-persistent test-time training method for a pretrained language model, measuring separately the adaptation within a document and the persistence of the fast weight across documents, against a reset at the document boundary and against a matched baseline, and to confirm the main effects on a test set fixed in advance. The report makes four contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A gated carry for In-Place TTT fast weights, with its own zero-initialised path and trained through the boundary between slices (Section~\ref{sec-design-method}). Against a reset, it lowers perplexity on the continuation of a document by about 1\%, replicated at two seeds (Section~\ref{ch-design}). An ablation at two seeds shows that removing the reset alone gains little, and that only the combination of the separate path and training through the boundary recovers the gain (Section~\ref{sec-ablation}).
  \item Evidence that transfer across documents is ordered by relatedness, confirmed on a preregistered test set at two seeds. Most of the gain is generic to the source, a small part depends on the relation between the documents, and text from another source raises perplexity (Sections~\ref{sec-related} and~\ref{sec-confirmatory}).
  \item Evidence at two seeds that training across document boundaries removes the harm, at the cost of the advantage of related text (Section~\ref{sec-mixed}).
  \item An analysis of where the gains come from. Most of the lead of a TTT model over a matched LoRA control is present with its fast weights switched off, a parameter-matched control shows that the gated carry still leads by 0.07 to 0.10, attention on the earlier text is a far stronger channel when that text is available, and the read-time clip that keeps the fast weights stable passes on only the direction of the state (Sections~\ref{ch-why} and~\ref{sec-compare}).
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{LITERATURE REVIEW}
\label{ch-lit}

This chapter reviews test-time training (Section~\ref{sec-lit-ttt}), fast weights and the update rules, gates and stabilisers that govern them (Section~\ref{sec-lit-fw}), how earlier models keep information across segments and documents (Section~\ref{sec-lit-persist}), dynamic evaluation and continual learning (Section~\ref{sec-lit-dyneval}) and evaluation methodology (Section~\ref{sec-lit-eval}). It closes by positioning this study (Section~\ref{sec-lit-gap}).

\section{Test-time training}
\label{sec-lit-ttt}

Test-time training was introduced by \citet{sun2020ttt} for image classification under distribution shift. A shared feature extractor takes a few gradient steps on a self-supervised loss of the single test input, the prediction is made with the updated extractor, and the update is discarded before the next input. The authors give a sufficient condition for this to help in the convex case, namely that the gradients of the self-supervised and main losses are positively correlated. An online variant starts each input from the extractor adapted on the previous one, but assumes a stream from one fixed or slowly changing distribution. Later work in vision kept the reset. \citet{gandelsman2022ttt} discard the adapted weights after every input and show that a misaligned self-supervised signal can lower accuracy. Continual test-time adaptation drops the reset entirely. In the formulation surveyed by \citet{maharana2026continual}, a model is updated online over a sequence of target distributions and never reset, the two failure modes are forgetting and the accumulation of errors from noisy self-generated targets, and stochastic restoration of part of the weights bounds long-horizon drift. Persistence is therefore possible, but it has to be bounded.

In language models, \citet{sun2025learning} make the hidden state of a sequence layer itself a model, updated by a gradient step on a self-supervised loss for each token or mini-batch, and each sequence starts again from the learned initial state. Titans \citep{behrouz2025titans} train a neural memory online with momentum and an adaptive weight decay that acts as a forget gate, within a single context per example. \citet{tandon2025endtoend} meta-learn the initial state of fast weights in the MLPs of the last quarter of the blocks, and discard training documents shorter than 8K tokens specifically to avoid resetting at packed document boundaries. On needle-in-a-haystack retrieval, full attention outperforms every test-time training method they test. Query-only TTT \citep{bansal2025letsnot} updates the query projections within one prompt, and \citet{akyurek2025surprising} train a LoRA adapter per task and discard it, finding that a shared adapter helps on some task families and interferes on others.

In-Place TTT \citep{feng2026inplace} is the starting point of this report. It repurposes the down-projection of a gated MLP as the fast weight, so it can be added to a pretrained model without a new layer. For each chunk it applies the current fast weight and then updates it with an outer product of a target and the MLP activation, a Hebbian accumulation with no error term. The target is a causal depthwise convolution of the input followed by a learned projection, aligned with next-token prediction. For long sequences, each chunk's update is clipped by its Frobenius norm for stability. In-Place TTT improves the RULER accuracy of Qwen3-4B at long context, and at document boundaries the fast weights are reset to prevent leakage between independent sequences. No partial carry or decay is tested. \citet{wang2026learning} replace the target with test-time context distillation, an error-correcting write, and \citet{ouyang2026ttt} use the layer's own next-position hidden state as the target. Under matched continual pretraining they find that the original target does not improve RULER over the backbone, while theirs does, for four models including Qwen3-0.6B. Both stay within one input, and \citet{ouyang2026ttt} restore the weights after every sample.

Test-time training differs from in-context learning, which adapts a frozen model through its context alone and is bounded by the context window \citep{brown2020language,dong2024survey}. In the evaluation of this report each slice is read without the previous text in attention, so the carried state is the only channel between slices, and a gain from carrying it cannot come from in-context learning over earlier text.

\section{Fast weights, update rules and stability}
\label{sec-lit-fw}

\citet{ba2016using} revive fast weights in recurrent networks with a decayed outer product of the hidden state, $A(t) = \lambda A(t-1) + \eta\, h(t) h(t)^\top$, and need layer normalisation to keep it stable. Causal linear attention is a recurrent network whose state adds the outer product of each key and value \citep{katharopoulos2020transformers}, and \citet{schlag2021linear} identify this state as a fast-weight matrix. The fast weight of In-Place TTT belongs to this family. Read as an associative memory, such a state has limited capacity. An additive matrix memory holds at most as many associations as its key dimension without retrieval error \citep{schlag2021linear}, and under the Hebbian rule the number of storable associations is bounded by the rank of the state \citep{mcdermott2026lifelong}.

The update rule decides how the state behaves when it keeps being written. The additive, or Hebbian, rule adds every outer product in full and cannot overwrite a stale association. The delta rule writes the difference between the new value and the one currently retrieved, and lowers WikiText-103 perplexity by 1.5 to 2.8 points at matched size \citep{schlag2021linear}. \citet{yang2024deltanet} derive it as one step of online gradient descent on a regression loss, so the update scales with the prediction error, and with normalised keys its state cannot grow without bound. Error correction is not uniformly beneficial. Fine-tuning GPT-2 into fast-weight models, \citet{mao2022finetuning} finds the additive rule slightly ahead of the delta rule under the best configuration, and the delta rule diverging unless its features are normalised and its gate starts very small. \citet{yang2025gated} find decay and the delta rule complementary.

A gate or a decay lets a state forget. In a controlled ablation, linear attention with no gate reaches a training perplexity of 23.21, against 16.55 with a fixed decay and 14.77 with a fine-grained gate \citep{yang2024gla}. Mamba makes its state update depend on the input, and notes that when documents are packed together a time-invariant model bleeds information from one sequence into the next, whereas a selective model can learn to reset its state \citep{gu2024mamba}. The mLSTM writes its matrix memory with learned forget and input gates \citep{beck2024xlstm}, and the RWKV authors note that funnelling all past context through one fixed-size state limits the recall of fine detail \citep{peng2023rwkv}.

Every family needs a mechanism that keeps its state in a usable range. Linear attention divides its read by an accumulated normaliser \citep{katharopoulos2020transformers}, recurrent fast weights use layer normalisation \citep{ba2016using}, TTT layers wrap their inner model in normalisation and a residual \citep{sun2025learning}, and In-Place TTT clips each update \citep{feng2026inplace}. Large-chunk TTT normalises the fast weights after every update against magnitude explosion \citep{zhang2025test}, and Generative Adapter resets all singular values of its outer-product update to one \citep{chen2024generative}. A decay or a pull-back keeps the relative scale of recent writes, while a norm bound applied to a large state keeps only its direction, which is the property Section~\ref{ch-why} examines. Large-chunk TTT and Generative Adapter keep only direction and still work, so discarding magnitude does not by itself make a fast weight useless.

\section{Persistence across segments and documents}
\label{sec-lit-persist}

Several architectures keep information beyond the current segment. Transformer-XL \citep{dai2019transformerxl} caches the previous segment's hidden states for the next segment to attend to, so what persists is a cache of activations and no parameter is updated. The Compressive Transformer \citep{rae2019compressive} compresses old activations into a second memory and is trained without resetting it at article boundaries. The Recurrent Memory Transformer \citep{bulatov2022rmt} passes memory tokens between segments and trains them by backpropagation through several segments, and deeper unrolling is what makes the memory useful. Memorizing Transformers \citep{wu2022memorizing} retrieve past keys and values by nearest-neighbour search and clear this memory at the start of each new document.

Other methods compress earlier text into a compact state that a later input can use. AutoCompressors \citep{chevalier2023adapting} turn each segment into summary vectors read as soft prompts, trained with gradients stopped after two compression steps, and although they are trained within documents, the vectors of separately retrieved documents can be combined and still help. Infini-attention \citep{munkhdalai2024leave} keeps a compressive associative memory across the segments of one input. MemoryLLM \citep{wang2024memoryllm} keeps a pool of memory tokens in every layer, updated by forward passes over a stream of texts with designed forgetting, and Generative Adapter \citep{chen2024generative} maps a stream of context chunks to a low-rank weight update in a single forward pass. Where such methods are compared with keeping the full text in context, the text often remains more accurate. On multi-session conversations, prompting with the full history reaches an F1 of 66.0 against 40.2 for Generative Adapter.

Persistence of fast weights is rarer. \citet{schlag2021linear} carry the fast-weight state across the training segments of WikiText-103. With the additive rule this breaks, reaching a perplexity above 1600, while the delta rule carries stably. \citet{irie2022modern} find that resetting self-modifying weights after every fixed span removes their benefit. In test-time training the reset is the rule. Per-sample resets are a stated design choice in vision \citep{sun2020ttt,gandelsman2022ttt}, TTT layers reset per sequence \citep{sun2025learning}, In-Place TTT and end-to-end TTT reset at document boundaries \citep{feng2026inplace,tandon2025endtoend}, and Titans and context distillation stay within one context \citep{behrouz2025titans,wang2026learning}. \citet{tiezzi2025state} note that learning online from an unsegmented stream without resets remains largely unexplored for recurrent models. \citet{kim2026delayed} argue that next-token training gives a test-time memory little reason to keep information through many later updates, and add a delayed loss whose gradient reaches the earlier writes.

\section{Dynamic evaluation and continual learning}
\label{sec-lit-dyneval}

Dynamic evaluation \citep{krause2018dynamic} adapts a model's parameters by gradient descent on each segment of the test sequence as it is read. Its best update rescales gradients with training statistics and decays the parameters back towards their trained values, and a sparse variant that adapts a small matrix recovers most of the gain. The benefit rises and then levels off within a few thousand characters of in-distribution text. \citet{lazaridou2021mind} apply it to temporal drift. It narrows the gap to a model trained on newer data, but forgets older data as it adapts. Their analysis of new words also finds that a second occurrence becomes much easier only while the first is still in the attention cache. Dynamic evaluation therefore already carries adaptation from earlier text, but by gradient steps on the slow weights rather than by a fast weight built in the forward pass.

A state carried from one document to the next is a form of continual learning and inherits its central problem, catastrophic forgetting in shared distributed weights, the stability and plasticity dilemma \citep{french1999catastrophic}. In large language models, forgetting during sequential fine-tuning grows with model size \citep{li2024revisiting}. \citet{oneill2026continually} write invented facts one at a time into a model. After twenty writes it answers between 1 and 46 percent of questions about earlier facts, depending mostly on how the later facts were written, and an earlier fact's raised log-probability can remain after the model no longer answers questions about it. Surveys of continual learning for language models organise the field by training-time updates \citep{shi2024continual,zheng2024towards} and do not treat test-time parameter adaptation.

\section{Evaluation methodology}
\label{sec-lit-eval}

Perplexity on held-out text is the standard measure in this literature \citep{krause2018dynamic,lazaridou2021mind}, but it has known limits. \citet{meister2021beyond} warn that with large samples almost every test is significant even for trivial effects. Long-context benchmarks such as RULER \citep{hsieh2024ruler} and LongBench \citep{bai2023longbench} probe the use of context more directly. \citet{song2026beyond} argue that perplexity answers a narrower question than claims of memory. They separate stream or domain adaptation, measured by future-token loss, from bridge evidence that information is stored in model state and from behaviour after the original context is removed, and call the use of lower-level evidence for a higher-level claim evidence migration. They place In-Place TTT and related layers at the first level. In a controlled test, one-step updates lowered the loss on injected facts at three model scales while free-form recall stayed at zero. They recommend matched baselines for such claims, including a model without updates and the same text given in the prompt.

Three lessons shape the protocol of this report. A gain must be attributed to the mechanism that produces it, as the fragmentation ablation of Transformer-XL shows \citep{dai2019transformerxl}, so the evaluation separates training gain, within-document gain and carried gain in paired conditions. Effects in this area are small, so intervals and win rates are reported rather than significance alone \citep{meister2021beyond}. And persistence must be tested at the boundaries where it is supposed to act, which earlier methods either avoid by clearing their memory per document \citep{wu2022memorizing} or observe only in passing.

\label{sec-lit-gap}

Three patterns emerge. First, every test-time training method for language models resets its adapted state at document boundaries or never leaves a single context. Where persistence across inputs has been studied, it either assumes a homogeneous stream, carries activations rather than a fast weight, or carries slow weights updated by gradient steps.

Second, the update-rule literature predicts that a carried fast weight will be hard to keep useful. A purely additive state accumulates interference and cannot overwrite \citep{schlag2021linear,yang2024deltanet}, an ungated one cannot forget \citep{yang2024gla,mao2022finetuning}, and an adapted model that is never reset accumulates errors \citep{maharana2026continual}. The designs that work add decay, gating, error correction or a training signal that crosses the boundary \citep{yang2025gated,bulatov2022rmt,chevalier2023adapting,kim2026delayed}.

Third, whether adaptation on one text helps another depends on how the two relate. Test-time training provably helps in the convex case when its gradient is aligned with that of the main task \citep{sun2020ttt} and can hurt when its signal is misaligned \citep{gandelsman2022ttt}. Fine-tuning on retrieved neighbours of an input improves language modelling, and the order from nearest to farthest neighbour matters \citep{hardt2024test}. In pre-training on SlimPajama, packing related documents together lowers perplexity, packing documents of one source helps less, and unrelated earlier documents distract the model \citep{zhao2024analysing}. That transfer follows relatedness is therefore expected, and the open question is whether a carried fast weight shows it.

The gap is therefore specific. To our knowledge, no prior work gives a TTT fast weight a trained carry path, measures what the carry contributes against a reset at the document boundary, or asks how its effect depends on the relatedness of the earlier text. This report adds a gated carry to In-Place TTT, compares it with carrying the state through the existing path under a Hebbian and a delta update rule, and measures within-document and cross-document effects separately with paired conditions and bootstrap intervals.

\chapter{METHODOLOGY}
\label{ch-method}
\label{ch-eval}

We extend a pretrained language model with In-Place TTT layers and a gated carry, train it alongside a matched control, and compare them on held-out text in paired evaluation conditions that isolate each source of gain. The models are implemented with PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers and PEFT.

\section{Data Collection}

Two public corpora are used. Training and the main evaluation use the SlimPajama-6B subset \citep{yoon2023slimpajama6b} of SlimPajama \citep{soboleva2023slimpajama}, a cleaned and deduplicated derivative of RedPajama-V1 \citep{weber2024redpajama}, restricted to six RedPajama sources, namely ArXiv, Book, C4, Github, StackExchange and Wikipedia. Transfer between related documents is measured on English Wikipedia articles from the November 2023 dump, whose title mentions link related articles. Table~\ref{tab-data} outlines the data.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Outline of the data.}

\section{Data Preprocessing}

Training uses 4000 documents of at least 2048 tokens, truncated to 16384 tokens and balanced across sources towards a target mix of 25 percent C4, 20 percent each of Github and ArXiv, 15 percent Book, and 10 percent each of Wikipedia and StackExchange. Except in one probe (Section~\ref{sec-mixed}), each session is one document, read whole if shorter than 2100 tokens and otherwise split into two to six slices of at least 1000 tokens.

The held-out set is drawn from documents never used in training. It is drawn from the last 15{,}000 rows of the dataset, which are removed before any training stage, and long enough to cut into slices. Every model is evaluated on exactly the same documents.

Wikipedia articles of at least 6000 characters serve as primes and those of at least 12{,}000 characters as targets. Each article is tokenised with the model's tokenizer and cut into blocks of 1024 tokens, the target's second block is the text that is scored, and the primes are 1024-token blocks chosen as described in Section~\ref{sec-relatedness-method}. For the confirmatory test set, articles whose opening text shares at least five ten-word sequences with any Wikipedia document in the training data are removed, and documents of the development holdout are removed from the confirmatory holdout.

\section{Models and training}

All models start from the pretrained Qwen3-0.6B model. TTT layers replace the MLPs of the 14 odd-numbered layers, 1, 3 and so on up to 27.

\subsection{In-Place TTT on the MLP down-projection}

Every transformer block applies a position-wise feed-forward MLP after attention \citep{vaswani2017attention}, and In-Place TTT \citep{feng2026inplace} uses the final, down-projection matrix of this MLP as its fast weight. Let $z \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{ff}}$ be the gated activation of a transformer MLP and $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d_{ff}}$ its down-projection. In-Place TTT adds a fast weight $S$ of the same shape, built during the forward pass, and a learned scalar gate $g(h) = \sigma(w_g^\top h + b_g)$ computed from the layer input $h$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{out} = W_0 z + g(h)\, \eta\, S z .
  \label{eq-out}
\end{equation}
The fast weight is written in chunks of $C$ tokens. For chunk $k$ the layer forms a target $V_k$ from a causal depthwise convolution of the normalised hidden states followed by a learned projection $W_{\mathrm{target}}$. $W_{\mathrm{target}}$ is zero-initialised, so every TTT layer is identical to the base MLP at the start of training. Chunk $k$ reads only the state accumulated from strictly earlier chunks, which keeps the layer causal. Figure~\ref{fig-arch} shows the layer.

\subsection{Update rules}

\paragraph{Hebbian.} The Hebbian rule, the form used by In-Place TTT and by linear attention \citep{feng2026inplace,katharopoulos2020transformers}, writes an outer product with no error term,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta S_k = \tfrac{1}{C} V_k^\top Z_k, \qquad S_k = S_{\mathrm{carried}} + \textstyle\sum_{j<k} \Delta S_j .
\end{equation}
Nothing in this write checks whether $S$ already predicts $V$, so the state grows with every chunk.

\paragraph{Chunked delta.} The chunked delta rule writes the residual of the current prediction, with the state frozen at the start of each chunk,
\begin{equation}
  \hat V_k = \frac{\eta}{1 + \lVert z \rVert^2}\, Z_k S_k^\top, \qquad \Delta S_k = \tfrac{1}{C} (V_k - \hat V_k)^\top Z_k ,
\end{equation}
with the normalised step $\eta / (1 + \lVert z \rVert^2)$ applied per token row. For this rule the read in Equation~\ref{eq-out} uses the same normalised step, so the TTT term is $\hat V_k$. The delta rule was introduced for fast weights by \citet{schlag2021linear} and amounts to a step of online gradient descent on a regression loss \citep{yang2024deltanet}. This is the mini-batch form of the delta rule used by \citet{sun2025learning}, and its error term lets a state that already predicts the target stop growing.

\subsection{Session persistence}

A session is a sequence of work items, either whole documents or slices of one document. At the end of each item the layer folds the item's total update into a carried state with an exponential moving average,
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{carried}} \leftarrow \lambda\, S_{\mathrm{carried}} + \Delta S_{\mathrm{item}}, \qquad \lambda = 0.9 ,
\end{equation}
and the next item starts from $S_{\mathrm{carried}}$ instead of from zero. This is the decayed accumulation that \citet{ba2016using} use for fast weights within a sequence, applied here between items. In the ungated carry this state is detached between items, so the layer learns to read it but is never trained on what it writes for the next item. The gated carry of Section~\ref{sec-design-method} reads it through a separate path and keeps it in the graph for two items. During training every session is a single document, except in the probe of Section~\ref{sec-mixed}, so the carry is learned only between the slices of one document. Carrying it across documents is applied at evaluation, as a test of whether a state learned within documents transfers to the next one. Figure~\ref{fig-persist} contrasts this with resetting.

\subsection{Stabilisation}

Two mechanisms keep the fast weights stable. A Frobenius norm clip rescales the state whenever it is read, so that $\lVert \eta S \rVert_F \le \tau$ with $\tau = 20$, while preserving its direction. In-Place TTT similarly bounds the Frobenius norm of each chunk's update \citep{feng2026inplace}, while here the bound is applied to the state when it is read. For the Hebbian rule the clip acts only on the read, while for the delta rule the clipped state is also what the next chunk accumulates onto. The output gate is initialised with bias $b_g = -2$, except in the gated carry, so the TTT term enters at about 12 percent strength at the start of training and the model can learn to open or close it. Separately, the gradients of all trainable parameters are clipped to a total norm of 10. The read-time clip bounds the forward pass, including at evaluation where no gradients exist, while the gradient clip bounds each optimiser step. Section~\ref{ch-why} returns to the role of the read-time clip.

\subsection{Training objective and parameters}

All models are trained with the standard next-token cross-entropy. LoRA adapters \citep{hu2022lora} are trained in every model, so that the TTT models and the control differ only in the TTT layers. The TTT models additionally train the down-projection $W_0$ of the TTT layers and the new modules (target projection, convolution and gate). Counted from the layer shapes of Qwen3-0.6B, this gives about 68.0 million trainable parameters for a TTT model (9.2 million LoRA, 44.0 million in the 14 down-projections, 14.8 million in the new modules) against about 10.1 million for the LoRA-only control. This difference matters for interpreting the results and is discussed in Sections~\ref{sec-compare} and~\ref{sec-limits}.

All runs use LoRA of rank 16 with $\alpha = 32$ and dropout 0.05, a LoRA learning rate of $10^{-5}$, AdamW, 16 gradient accumulation steps, a warmup of 2 percent of steps, cosine decay over the full epoch of 1050 optimiser steps, and seed 42. TTT runs train their down-projections at $3 \times 10^{-5}$ and their new modules at $6 \times 10^{-2}$. Checkpoints at steps 200, 400 and 600 are evaluated, and one gated-carry run at seed 42 is trained to the end of the schedule, which the data ends at step 1049. Because the epoch length depends only on the data and the session strategy, all seed-42 runs share the same 1050-step schedule and data order. Seed 43 selects different documents, giving 1048 steps, and the probes of Section~\ref{sec-mixed} keep the documents and step count of their seed but group them into different sessions. Appendix~\ref{app-hparams} lists every setting.

\subsection{The gated carry}
\label{sec-design-method}

Simply starting each item from the carried state, the ungated carry, leaves three problems. The state is detached between items, so nothing trains what an earlier item writes for a later one to read. The carried state enters the same sum as the current item's state and is clipped with it, so the current item can dominate it. And a Hebbian layer whose gate starts at 12 percent strength begins training with a loss spike during which its fast weights are unusable. The gated carry addresses each of these with one change, built for the Hebbian rule, with every other setting as in the ungated Hebbian model.

\paragraph{A separate carry path.} The carried state is read through its own term with its own clip, gate and scale,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{out} = W_0 z + g(h)\, \eta\, S_{\mathrm{in}} z + \alpha\, g_c(h)\, \eta\, \mathrm{clip}(S_{\mathrm{carried}})\, z ,
\end{equation}
where $S_{\mathrm{in}}$ is the state built inside the current slice, $g_c$ is a second gate and $\alpha$ is a learnable scalar initialised to zero. As with the zero-initialised factor of LoRA \citep{hu2022lora}, the layer is therefore exactly the In-Place TTT layer at initialisation, and the carried term enters only if training finds it useful.

\paragraph{Training through the carry.} The carried state is kept in the autograd graph between the slices of a session, so the loss on a later slice also trains what an earlier slice wrote. This follows the Recurrent Memory Transformer, whose memory improves substantially when it is trained by backpropagation across more segments \citep{bulatov2022rmt}. To bound memory, the carry is detached after every second slice and at every optimiser step. AutoCompressors make the same choice, stopping gradients after two compression steps with no loss of quality \citep{chevalier2023adapting}. Because gradient checkpointing re-runs each layer's forward pass during the backward pass, the recompute is given the carry the layer actually read, and we verified that gradients with and without checkpointing are identical.

\paragraph{A nearly closed main gate at the start.} The main gate's bias is initialised at $-6$ instead of $-2$, so the in-document fast weights start at about 0.25 percent strength rather than 12 percent. This is close to the value the gate of the ungated Hebbian model reaches after training (0.002).

\begin{description}
  \item[LoRA-only.] The matched control, with no TTT layers.
  \item[Gated carry.] TTT layers with the Hebbian rule and the gated carry, trained at seeds 42 and 43.
  \item[Ungated carry.] TTT layers whose state is carried through the existing path, with the Hebbian and with the chunked delta rule. These are the In-Place TTT layer with its reset removed, and serve as the ablation without the carry path.
  \item[Parameter-matched control.] The gated-carry architecture with the same trainable parameters, trained to step 600 with its fast weights never written, so that it isolates the effect of the extra parameters. Because in-training evaluation switches the fast weights on, the training loop restores this setting after every evaluation.
  \item[Ablations.] Three runs to step 200 at seeds 42 and 43 that remove components of the gated carry in turn (Section~\ref{sec-ablation}).
  \item[Probe trained across documents.] The gated carry trained on sessions of several documents, at seeds 42 and 43 (Section~\ref{sec-mixed-method}).
\end{description}

Differences between conditions give the decomposition reported throughout.
\begin{description}
  \item[Training gain.] Base minus TTT off. Everything the slow weights learned, including LoRA and, for TTT models, the trained $W_0$.
  \item[Within-document gain.] TTT off minus TTT reset per slice. What the fast weights learn inside a slice.
  \item[Between-slice gain.] TTT reset per slice minus TTT carried. What carrying state from earlier slices of the same document adds.
  \item[Matched gain.] The LoRA-only model minus the TTT model with its fast weights carried. The total advantage over the control.
\end{description}

\subsection{Persistence suites}

Three further suites probe persistence directly.
\begin{description}
  \item[Boundary.] For each source, a chain of five documents is read under three conditions, carrying state across documents, resetting at document boundaries, and resetting at every slice. The cross-document gain compares the first two conditions on documents two to five of each chain (24 documents), and the cross-slice gain compares the last two on all 30 documents. For the gated carry from step 200 onwards the chains have 20 documents per source, so the cross-document gain rests on 100 documents. The condition that resets at document boundaries follows the reset rule of In-Place TTT, so the cross-document gain compares carrying with resetting on the same model, data and documents. The reset condition is not a reproduction of In-Place TTT as published, which uses chunks of 512 or 1024 tokens, clips each chunk's update rather than the state, has no output gate and no LoRA, and was evaluated on models of 4B parameters and more \citep{feng2026inplace}.
  \item[Repeat.] Each document is read ten times in a row with its carried state never reset. Each later reading is compared with the first, and the tenth reading is also compared with the second, which tests whether the carry keeps compounding after one exposure. This is the most favourable test possible for a carried state, since the state was built from the very document being read again.
  \item[Swap.] A target document is read after the state has been primed on two documents from the same source or from a different source, each read whole, and compared with reading it unprimed, to test whether any carried effect is source-specific.
\end{description}

\subsection{Relatedness suite}
\label{sec-relatedness-method}

The boundary suite compares two points, the same document and a different document of the same source. To test documents that are distinct but related, a further suite uses English Wikipedia, a source on which the gated carry gains strongly between slices (Section~\ref{ch-q2}). The articles come from two shards of the November 2023 English dump. They may overlap the Wikipedia portion of the training data. Every condition scores the same target tokens, so an overlap affects the target alike in every condition, although a prime seen in training may act differently from an unseen one. For each of 200 target articles, chosen in a seeded order, the second block of 1024 tokens is scored after the carry is reset and after it is primed by a block of 1024 tokens from one of four texts, ordered by relatedness.
\begin{description}
  \item[Same document.] The target article's own first 1024 tokens.
  \item[Related article.] The article the target mentions most often by title, at least twice, matching whole multi-word titles only.
  \item[Random article.] An article that neither mentions the target nor is mentioned by it.
  \item[Other source.] A held-out document from one of the other five SlimPajama sources.
\end{description}
Every prime has the same length and every condition scores exactly the same target tokens, so only the prime varies. Because relatedness is defined by title mentions, a related prime also tends to share more words with the target, and no control here separates relatedness in topic from word overlap. The same 200 cases, identical title for title, were measured on the gated carry and on the ungated Hebbian model, both at step 600. These are development cases, and Section~\ref{sec-confirmatory} repeats the suite on confirmatory ones.

\subsection{Confirmatory test set fixed in advance}
\label{sec-confirmatory-method}

The development results come from the documents on which the design was developed. To test the design on data it was not tuned on, the relatedness suite was rerun on new cases with an analysis plan published before the run (a preregistration), which fixes the cases, the conditions, the contrasts and the rules that decide which claims may be made. The 200 cases come from two other shards of the same Wikipedia dump, with a different case seed, each related article used for one target only. A case is skipped if its target or primes share at least five ten-word sequences with any Wikipedia document in either seed's training data, which removed one case. Three conditions are added. A generic carry is the mean of the carried states of 200 further articles, rescaled in each layer to the median norm of a single carried state, and tests whether the gain after a related article is more than a generic effect of reading Wikipedia. An in-context condition reads the related prime and the target as one input with the carry reset, which is also the equal-memory baseline, since the prime is shorter than the 1500 tokens the carried state could hold as an attention cache. A chain condition carries 1, 2, 5 or 10 random articles in sequence before the target. Intervals use 10{,}000 bootstrap resamples over cases, and the four primary contrasts also get Bonferroni-adjusted 98.75 percent intervals. The other-source primes are drawn from 62 SlimPajama documents and so recur across cases, which the case-level bootstrap does not account for, so the intervals for that condition are somewhat too narrow. The main evaluation of Table~\ref{tab-conditions} was rerun on a new holdout draw from which the 106 development documents were removed. The holdout pool is small, so this left 73 documents from five sources and none from Book, a shortfall the plan did not anticipate. These results are therefore reported pooled over sources only.

\subsection{Probe trained across documents}
\label{sec-mixed-method}

The main models are trained only within documents, so their use across documents is a transfer test. To see what changes when the carry is trained across boundaries, a probe was trained at each seed with the same data, schedule and settings as the gated carry of that seed, except that each training session chains five documents, each next document from the same source with probability one half and otherwise from another source, with the carried state never reset inside a session. It was evaluated on the same confirmatory cases, and each contrast is its gain over its own reset carry minus that of the gated carry of the same seed, both resampled on the same cases.

\subsection{Statistics}

Perplexity within a document is token-weighted over its slices. A gap between two conditions is the difference of their geometric-mean perplexities over documents, positive when the second condition has lower perplexity. We report 95 percent percentile bootstrap intervals from 1000 resamples of documents (10{,}000 for the confirmatory test set of Section~\ref{sec-confirmatory}), with both conditions resampled on the same indices, and the win rate, the fraction of documents on which the second condition is lower. A star marks an interval that excludes zero. Following \citet{meister2021beyond}, every result is reported as an effect size with its interval rather than as a significance test alone.

\subsection{Sanity checks}

Every evaluation runs with dropout disabled, and the base condition restores the pretrained $W_0$. Two checks confirm the evaluation. The LoRA-only model, which has no fast weights, gives a within-document and between-slice gain of exactly 0.000 at every step, and the base-model perplexities are identical across all LoRA-only evaluations. In-training evaluations, which use 30 documents and no intervals, are reported only to describe training dynamics.

  \caption{Gain from carrying state from earlier slices of the same document, by slice, with 95 percent bootstrap bands.}
  \label{fig-design-slice}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Across documents a small benefit becomes a small harm.} With 100 documents, carrying state into a different document of the same source lowers perplexity by 0.010 $[+0.004, +0.018]$ at step 200, mainly on C4 and Wikipedia. By step 400 the effect is gone ($-0.003$), and at step 600 the carried state makes perplexity worse, by 0.020 $[-0.030, -0.013]$, on 71 percent of documents, with a significant harm on four of the six sources. Its gain on the continuation of the same document stays significant throughout. Because training sessions never cross a document boundary, this measures whether a carry learned within documents transfers across them.

\paragraph{The carry recovers about four percent of the gain of attention.} The context suite compares the carry with placing the previous slice directly in attention, scoring the same tokens. At step 600 the previous slice in attention lowers perplexity by 2.770 (25.7 percent), the carried state by 0.110, so the carry recovers 4.0 percent $[3.3, 4.7]$ of that gain, and 3.9 percent $[3.1, 4.6]$ at step 200. The carried state is a much weaker channel than attention when the text is still available, although it is not small. It holds 44.0 million values, which in the 32-bit precision used here take about 176 MB, as much as the 16-bit attention cache of roughly 1500 tokens of Qwen3-0.6B (28 layers, 8 key-value heads of dimension 128 under grouped-query attention \citep{ainslie2023gqa}). Whether it is useful where attention cannot reach, after the earlier text has left the context, is not tested.

\paragraph{The benefit flows through the carry path.} The main gate stays nearly closed (median 0.0007 at step 200), so in-slice fast weights contribute little and the gain comes through the carry path. The model leads the matched LoRA control by 0.234 $[0.188, 0.293]$ perplexity at step 600 on every document. About 0.11 of this lead is extra trainable capacity, since its training gain is 1.971 against 1.859 for the control (Section~\ref{sec-compare}).

\paragraph{The closed gate does not explain the gain.} With the closed gate alone, the carry between slices gives 0.008 and 0.040 at the two seeds, against 0.099 and 0.104 for the gated carry. Starting the gate nearly closed raises the lead over the control, from 0.073 to 0.130 at seed 42, but not the carry to the level of the full design.

\paragraph{Only the combination recovers the gain.} At both seeds only the full gated carry gives a large gain between slices, 0.099 and 0.104, and the largest lead over the control, 0.215 and 0.214. Every partial variant stays at or below 0.040. Their order is not stable across seeds. At seed 42 training through the carry alone gives the most (0.035) and the separate path 0.023, at seed 43 the closed gate alone gives 0.040, the separate path 0.034 and training through the carry 0.015. In-training evaluations of the partial variants also move by up to 0.05 between steps 50 and 200 within one run, so their ranking at step 200 is not informative, and the seed-42 reading that the two components are super-additive does not replicate. What replicates is that neither the separate path nor training through the carry recovers the gain on its own. A plausible account is that the separate path lets a carried state reach the output without being drowned by the current slice, and that training through the carry teaches the model what to write for a later slice to read, but the ablation does not establish this.

\paragraph{The closed gate and the zero-initialised scale are needed for stable training.} Two further configurations could not be trained usefully. With the separate path but without the zero-initialised scale, and with the gate bias at $-2$, carrying state between slices raised perplexity from 16.3 to 31.5 at step 100, worse on every source. The clip bounds the carried state at $\tau = 20$, about 4.2 to 5.3 times the largest singular value of the pretrained down-projection, so a clipped state concentrated in few directions adds about half the strength of $W_0$ along them even through a gate at 12 percent. With the scale but the gate bias still at $-2$, the in-slice Hebbian fast weights, not the carry, raised perplexity to 4703 at step 50 and 1024 at step 100, against 16.6 and 16.2 with them off, before the model recovered by step 150. Starting the gate nearly closed removes this spike.

\paragraph{Cross-document carry is small in most variants.} At step 200 the variants carry a small effect into a different document, between 0.003 and 0.014, except the closed gate alone at seed 43, at 0.038. We draw no conclusion from that single value.

The confirmatory cases therefore show the same sharpening as the development cases.

The per-target gains from attention, examined after the preregistered analysis, have a median of about $-0.14$ and a long upper tail reaching $+13.8$, so a related article in attention helps a few targets greatly and slightly harms most. It scores the target at positions 1024 to 2047 rather than from the start of the input, and the target's own fast weights are built on top of the prime. We therefore draw no conclusion from it beyond the preregistered one, that the mean difference between the carry and attention is not resolved.

\subsection{Training the carry across documents}
\label{sec-mixed}

\paragraph{The harm after other-source text disappears.} After text of another source the probe gains $+0.037$ where the gated carry trained within documents loses $0.084$, a change of $+0.120$ $[+0.103, +0.140]$. The chain of ten articles gains $+0.041$ $[+0.028, +0.054]$ more, and the related article $+0.027$ $[+0.013, +0.041]$ more. These meet the preregistered rule for the claim that training across documents removes the harm. The seed-43 probe, compared with the seed-43 gated carry, meets it too: other-source text gains 0.064 $[0.051, 0.077]$ more and no longer changes perplexity ($+0.004$, $[-0.005, +0.014]$), the chain of ten articles gains 0.039 $[0.027, 0.051]$ more and the related article 0.016 $[0.004, 0.028]$ more. The harm meant here is the harm after other-source text and after a chain on the confirmatory cases. The boundary suite was not run on the probes.

\paragraph{The carry becomes largely generic.} In the probe every condition gains about the same (Table~\ref{tab-confirmatory}). Related against the generic carry is $-0.002$ $[-0.007, +0.003]$, and the same article against the generic carry $+0.002$ $[-0.003, +0.007]$, so the part of the transfer that was specific to the document is gone. Within a document the carry between slices falls from $+0.125$ to $+0.073$, a change of $-0.052$ $[-0.078, -0.032]$. Other-source text still gains less, $+0.037$, so the carry is not independent of the source, but within Wikipedia it no longer distinguishes related from unrelated text. At seed 43 related against generic is $-0.001$ $[-0.007, +0.004]$, and the carry between slices falls from $+0.118$ to $+0.087$, a change of $-0.030$ $[-0.052, -0.014]$. Training across documents thus trades the harm for a largely generic carry at both seeds, and loses 26 to 40 percent of the gain the carry gives within a document. Both seeds use one mixing ratio.

\subsection{The saturated clip}
\label{ch-why}

\paragraph{The read-time clip is always active.} The clip binds whenever $\lVert \eta S \rVert_F > \tau$. In units of the pretrained down-projection, this is a state ratio $\lVert \eta S \rVert_F / \lVert W_0 \rVert_F$ above $\tau / \lVert W_0 \rVert_F = 0.43$, given a mean $\lVert W_0 \rVert_F$ of 46.6 over the TTT layers. Across every boundary, repeat, swap and main-evaluation measurement of the ungated models at steps 200, 400 and 600, the state ratio is above this threshold on 100 percent of measurements, by a factor of at least 255 for the Hebbian model and at least 1597 for the delta model (Table~\ref{tab-clip}, Figure~\ref{fig-dist}). For the gated carry at step 600, the carried state ratio has a median of about 8{,}000 for seed 42 and 15{,}000 for seed 43, and is above the threshold on every document by a factor of at least 6400.

\paragraph{Only the direction of the state reaches the output.} A saturated clip means that the magnitude of the state never reaches the output. What reaches it is a vector of norm $\tau$ pointing along the accumulated state. For the ungated models, re-reading a document grows the carried state by a factor of about 6.5 over ten readings, from a median state ratio of about 14{,}600 after the first reading to about 95{,}000 after the tenth for the delta model, and from about 2{,}600 to 16{,}900 for the Hebbian model, while perplexity stays flat (Figure~\ref{fig-repeat}, which starts at the second reading). Carrying state into a new document makes it about two to four times larger than a state reset at the boundary, again with no effect on perplexity (Figure~\ref{fig-chain}).

We trained both rules without the clip, keeping every other setting, the data order and the gradient clip (Figure~\ref{fig-noclip}). The Hebbian model collapses. A likely account is that without an error term and with a fixed step the Hebbian state grows with every chunk until it overwhelms the forward pass, but this one short run does not isolate the cause.

\paragraph{Summary.} The clip keeps the fast weights usable by reducing an unusably large state to a fixed scale, and it does so by discarding the state's magnitude. In two short single-seed runs, removing it made the fast weights either unstable or unused. A saturated clip does not by itself rule out a useful carry, since the gated carry works with the clip as saturated as in the ungated models. What differs is the direction that is carried. The ungated carry's write is never trained to serve a later read, while the gated carry's is, and a plausible reading, supported only by the ablation of Section~\ref{sec-ablation}, is that training through the carry shapes that direction. The other ingredient of the gated carry, the separate path, may matter for the same reason, because in the ungated carry the carried state is clipped together with the current slice's state, so only the direction of their sum reaches the output.

\section{Comparison of Results}
\label{sec-compare}

\subsection{Against the matched control}

Every TTT model leads the LoRA-only control at every checkpoint, the ungated Hebbian model by 0.073 to 0.106 perplexity, the ungated delta model by 0.191 to 0.232 and the gated carry by 0.188 to 0.234. Most of this lead is not test-time learning. A TTT model trains about 6.7 times as many parameters, and with its fast weights switched off its training gain already exceeds the control's. At step 600 the ungated Hebbian model's lead of 0.106 splits into 0.069 of extra training gain, 0.034 within slices and 0.002 between slices, and the ungated delta model's lead of 0.203 into 0.133, 0.058 and 0.012, so about two thirds is present with the fast weights switched off. For the gated carry about 0.11 of its 0.234 is extra training gain, and on the confirmatory holdout 0.120 and 0.108 of its leads of 0.257 and 0.233 remain with the fast weights off. The within-slice and between-slice gains are the measures that isolate the mechanism.

\subsection{Against a parameter-matched control}

The parameter-matched control measures the capacity effect directly (Table~\ref{tab-pmatched}). With the gated carry's architecture and trainable parameters but no fast-weight updates, its gain from fast weights is exactly zero, and it already leads the LoRA-only control by 0.146 on the development holdout and 0.158 on the confirmatory holdout. The gated carry still leads it at both seeds and on both holdouts, by 0.068 to 0.098, close to its own gain between slices. With its fast weights switched off the gated carry is slightly worse than the control, by 0.009 and 0.024, so training the fast weights does not buy better slow weights, and the gated carry's lead comes from using its fast weights at test time.

\subsection{Gated and ungated carry, and the two update rules}

The gated carry raises the gain between slices of a document from 0.014 and 0.002 for the ungated Hebbian model at steps 200 and 600 to 0.099 and 0.097, and from the ungated delta model's 0.012 at step 600. Its lead over the control at step 600, 0.234, and the ungated delta model's, 0.203, have overlapping intervals, so neither is shown to be ahead. Within a slice the delta rule adapts more than the Hebbian rule, by 9 times at step 200 and 1.7 times at step 600. On the relatedness cases only the gated carry shows a gradient, while the ungated Hebbian carry has no significant gain at any level of relatedness (Table~\ref{tab-related}).

\subsection{Carry, attention and dynamic evaluation}

Placing the preceding slice of the same document in attention lowers perplexity by 2.770, about 25 times the gated carry's gain of 0.110 on the same tokens (Section~\ref{ch-design}). On the confirmatory relatedness cases, attention on a related article has a larger mean gain than the carry but is not cleanly comparable (Section~\ref{sec-confirmatory}). Dynamic evaluation of the LoRA-only model, tuned on development cases, gains more than the carry on the same document and less after a related or unrelated article (Appendix~\ref{app-dyneval}). It is a different model with different slow weights, so it supports no comparison claim.

Taken together, the same mechanism in the same model helps on the continuation of the same document throughout training, transfers to a distinct but related document, and harms when the next text comes from another source or, in the boundary suite, follows a chain of other whole documents. The relatedness suite turns this into a graded result, which replicates on preregistered confirmatory cases. Most of the gain after a Wikipedia article is generic, and a small part is specific to the document. This agrees with the swap result of Section~\ref{ch-q1}, where a state primed on a different source made perplexity worse even for the ungated Hebbian model, and with the distraction by unrelated earlier documents that \citet{zhao2024analysing} find in attention. It also suggests that a carry needs a way to decide when to apply itself, such as resetting on a change of topic, in the way a selective state-space model can learn to reset its state at a boundary \citep{gu2024mamba}.

\chapter{DISCUSSION}
\label{ch-discussion}

\section{Relation to the update-rule literature}

The delta rule's larger within-slice gain matches most of the literature, although \citet{mao2022finetuning} found the additive rule slightly ahead when fine-tuning GPT-2. An additive store saturates through interference and cannot overwrite stale associations \citep{schlag2021linear,yang2024deltanet}, and \citet{wang2026learning} find an error-correcting write better than In-Place TTT's additive one. Here the delta rule gives a within-slice gain up to nine times larger, and only the Hebbian model collapses without the clip, as \citet{schlag2021linear} observe for the additive rule without normalisation. The gate closing on the Hebbian model echoes \citet{yang2024gla}, where an ungated additive accumulator trains poorly. Yet for persistence the update rule matters little. The ungated carry gains little with either rule, and the gated carry, built on the weaker Hebbian rule, carries far more. The saturated clip also has a counterpart in the literature. Normalised reads, decays and the bounded transition of the delta rule keep a state in range as part of the update \citep{katharopoulos2020transformers,yang2024gla,yang2024deltanet}, whereas a clip applied at read time bounds it from outside and keeps only its direction.

\section{Relation to persistence and continual learning}

The ungated carry has a fixed decay, no selectivity and no training signal for what it writes. Persistence mechanisms that work avoid this in different ways. The Recurrent Memory Transformer trains its memory through several segments \citep{bulatov2022rmt}, dynamic evaluation decays its updates towards the trained weights \citep{krause2018dynamic}, and gated and selective updates let a model choose what to keep \citep{gu2024mamba,yang2025gated}. The gated carry adopts the first and third of these within documents, but its main models are never trained across documents. When a probe is trained across them, on a mix of same-source and other-source boundaries, the harm disappears but the carry stops distinguishing related from unrelated text. A plausible reading is that the probe found a carry that is harmless at both kinds of boundary more easily than one that tells them apart. The harm after a chain of documents in the boundary suite fits the continual-learning picture, in which a model that is never reset accumulates errors unless part of it is restored \citep{maharana2026continual}, and retention of an earlier write depends on how the later writes are made \citep{oneill2026continually}. The ungated carry's weak result also sits beside the finding of \citet{ouyang2026ttt} that the original In-Place TTT target does not improve RULER under matched training, which suggests that small gains from this layer depend heavily on what it writes.

\section{Related versus unrelated text}

The results separate two questions. Whether a carried fast weight can lower perplexity on later text is answered yes, but substantially only when it is read through its own path and its write is trained for a later read. Whether it then transfers beyond the document is answered by the relatedness gradient, which is the ordering already seen elsewhere. In pre-training, related earlier documents in attention help and unrelated ones distract \citep{zhao2024analysing}, test-time training on retrieved neighbours helps \citep{hardt2024test}, and a test-time update helps when its gradient is aligned with the main task and can hurt when it is not \citep{sun2020ttt,gandelsman2022ttt}. One reading, not tested here, is that a carry built from related text acts as an update aligned with the target and one built from another source as a misaligned one, with interference in shared weights \citep{french1999catastrophic} as a natural account of the harm.

Two further results fit the literature. That the carry does not accumulate with repeated reading resembles the levelling off of dynamic evaluation within a few thousand characters of in-distribution text \citep{krause2018dynamic}. And the far larger gain from placing the preceding text in attention matches the finding that full attention retrieves much better than test-time training \citep{tandon2025endtoend}, and the result for Generative Adapter, a weight update built in a forward pass, which reaches an F1 of 40.2 on multi-session conversations against 66.0 for prompting with the full history \citep{chen2024generative}.

\section{Implications}

Within the scope of these perplexity measurements, a carried fast weight helps after related text and should be reset when the source changes. Across unrelated documents of the same source the evidence is mixed, since a single article opening helps and a chain of whole documents harms at step 600. Within a document, attention remains far stronger when the earlier text is available. For research on persistent test-time learning, the results suggest that the write of a carried state has to be trained for a later read, and that this can work even when a read-time clip discards the state's magnitude. A training signal that spans document boundaries removes the harm in our probe, but at the cost of specificity, so such a signal needs a way to keep related and unrelated text apart, for example a learned reset or a gate that sees both documents.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec-limits}

\paragraph{Capacity.} The TTT models train about 6.7 times as many parameters as the LoRA-only control, and most of their lead over it is this extra capacity. The parameter-matched control separates the two, but it was trained only to step 600 and for the gated-carry architecture, so the lead beyond capacity is measured at step 600 and not for the ungated models.

\paragraph{Carry trained within documents only.} Every training session of the main models is one document, so they learn to use a carried state between slices of a document but are never trained with a state carried from a different document. The cross-document results therefore measure whether a within-document carry transfers across documents. Probes trained on multi-document sessions are reported at two seeds (Section~\ref{sec-mixed}), with one mixing ratio and sessions of five documents, so what such training learns in general is not established.

\paragraph{Scale.} One model of 0.6 billion parameters is studied. The gated carry, its ablation and the probes have two seeds, the ungated models one, and only one gated-carry run, at seed 42, is trained to the end of the schedule. The ungated carry's cross-document null result is stable across 200, 400 and 600 steps. The gated carry's cross-document effect moves from $+0.010$ at step 200 to $-0.020$ at step 600 and $-0.040$ at step 1049, consistently with its relatedness results becoming more specific, but the end-of-schedule result rests on one seed. Larger models are untested. Forgetting under sequential fine-tuning has been found to grow with model size \citep{li2024revisiting}, so the balance between transfer and interference may shift at larger scale.

\paragraph{Evaluation set.} The development holdout has 106 documents, with only 6 from Book. For the ungated models the cross-document test rests on 24 documents, about four per source, so it can rule out only effects larger than about 0.01. The gated carry's boundary suite uses 100.

\paragraph{Runs without the clip.} The runs without the clip are short, 100 to 200 steps, and single-seed. They show that removing the clip does not help early in training, not what a long unclipped run would converge to.

\paragraph{Gated carry.} The gated carry is replicated with two seeds, which is enough to show its results are not a chance result but not to report a spread, and it exists for the Hebbian rule only. Its ablation stops at step 200, at two seeds whose partial variants rank differently, while the relatedness results come from step 600, so which components the relatedness effects depend on is not tested. Its relatedness gradient is measured on Wikipedia only, with relatedness defined by title mentions, which includes word overlap, so the results do not separate relatedness in topic from shared words. The context comparison gives attention one previous slice, while the carry holds a decayed summary of all earlier slices, and the dynamic-evaluation baseline of Appendix~\ref{app-dyneval} uses a sparse form on the LoRA weights of a different model, the LoRA-only control. The in-context condition of Section~\ref{sec-confirmatory} reads the prime and the target as one input of 2048 tokens, so it also changes the position of the scored tokens, and its heavy-tailed gains were examined only after the preregistered analysis. The gap between the boundary and relatedness suites remains unexplained, since accumulation over a chain of Wikipedia articles does not reproduce it.

\paragraph{Development and confirmatory sets.} The gated carry, its gate bias of $-6$ and the choice of suites were all settled while looking at the same 106 SlimPajama documents and 200 Wikipedia cases. The relatedness results, the generic, in-context and chain conditions, and the pooled gains of the main evaluation are replicated on the preregistered confirmatory set (Section~\ref{sec-confirmatory}). The boundary, repeat, context and per-source results rest on the development set only. The confirmatory SlimPajama holdout has 73 documents from five sources and none from Book, because the reserved pool of long documents is small, so per-source results of the main evaluation could not be checked. The confirmatory Wikipedia cases were checked against the Wikipedia part of the training data by shared ten-word sequences, which removed one case, but a check on the decoded first 700 tokens of each training document cannot rule out every overlap. The confirmatory set tests the same model checkpoints, so it guards against selecting results on the development cases but not against design choices that suit Wikipedia in general.

\paragraph{Perplexity only.} All evidence is perplexity-based, and perplexity alone does not show how generated text or downstream behaviour changes \citep{meister2021beyond}. Lower loss can coexist with no behavioural recall \citep{song2026beyond}, and a written fact can leave a raised log-probability after the model stops using it \citep{oneill2026continually}, so our results support claims about stream and domain adaptation, not about memory. No retrieval benchmark such as RULER \citep{hsieh2024ruler} and no behavioural test of retained information was run, and a diagnostic that withholds the earlier text, in the spirit of LongBench \citep{bai2023longbench}, would help separate what is carried from what the base model already knows. Whether the carry helps where attention cannot reach, for example after the previous document has left the context, is not tested.

\chapter{CONCLUSION}
\label{ch-conclusion}

This report asked whether the fast weights that test-time training builds while reading one document can usefully be carried into the next. On Q1, removing the reset alone contributes little, at most 0.027 within a document and nothing measurable across documents, with either update rule. A gated carry, read through its own zero-initialised path and trained through the boundary between slices, lowers perplexity on the continuation of a document by about 1 percent, at two seeds and on the confirmatory holdout. Across documents its contribution is small and its sign depends on how the documents relate. It falls steadily from the same article to a related article to an unrelated one of the same source, and turns to harm for text from another source, and this holds across two training seeds and on a confirmatory test set analysed by a plan fixed in advance. Most of the benefit is a generic effect of the source, and a small part is specific to the relation between the documents. Training the carry across documents removes the harm but also that specific part, at two seeds. Trained to the end of its schedule, the carry becomes more specific: a related article's lead over the generic carry doubles while unrelated text harms more. Against a control with the same parameters, the gated carry still leads by 0.07 to 0.10. Repeated reading adds nothing beyond the second reading, and next to attention on the preceding text the carry recovers only about four percent of the gain.

On Q2, within a document the gated carry gains most, relative to perplexity, on C4, StackExchange and Wikipedia, and least on ArXiv, Github and Book, and the difficulty of a source does not predict the gain. Across documents the answer depends on the setup. At steps 600 and 1049 a chain of earlier documents of the same source harms, while on Wikipedia a single related or unrelated article helps.

Along the way, the attribution shows that most of a TTT model's lead over a matched control is already present with its fast weights switched off, which points to its extra trainable capacity, that within a slice the delta rule adapts 1.7 to 9 times more than the Hebbian rule, and that the read-time clip that keeps the fast weights stable passes on only the direction of the state, so the gated carry works by shaping that direction rather than its size. The open questions are whether the gradient holds to the end of training, beyond Wikipedia and at larger scale, why a chain of documents harms in one suite and helps in another, and how a model can learn when to apply its carry without losing what makes it specific to a document.

\section{Future research}

For the gated carry, the most direct extensions are further seeds at the full schedule to report a spread, the relatedness gradient on other domains and with measures of relatedness that separate topic from word overlap, and the same design for the delta rule. The most important open question is how a carry trained across document boundaries can keep the document-specific part of the transfer, for example through a learned reset or a gate that sees both documents, which needs further mixing ratios. More broadly, the extensions are a parameter-matched control at the full schedule, dynamic evaluation of the gated-carry model itself, a carried state with a designed bound such as a normalised memory, a learned rather than fixed decay of the carried state \citep{ravi2017optimization,yang2024gla}, behavioural tests of what the carried state retains after the earlier text has left the context, and replication at larger scale.

\section{Recommendations}

Three recommendations follow from the results. A carried fast weight should be reset when the source of the text changes, and kept only where the earlier text is known to be related, since across unrelated documents of the same source the two suites disagree. The write of a carried state should be trained for a later read, rather than left to the within-document objective. And claims about persistent test-time learning should be tested on data fixed in advance, against a generic carry and an in-context baseline, because most of a perplexity gain can be generic.

\section{Dynamic evaluation baseline}
\label{app-dyneval}

This baseline is reported for completeness and supports no claim in the main text. It runs on the LoRA-only model rather than on the gated-carry model, so the two conditions also differ in their slow weights. It adapts the LoRA weights with Adam and no decay, unlike the method of \citet{krause2018dynamic}, and it was tuned on development cases.

The LoRA-only model at step 600 takes Adam steps on its 10.1 million LoRA parameters over each prime, reads the target, and is restored exactly before the next case, with updates made on 32-bit copies. Step size and number of steps were tuned on 40 further Wikipedia cases, disjoint from the 200 reported, separately for the same-document and the related-article gain, over step sizes from $3 \times 10^{-6}$ to $10^{-4}$ and 3, 10 or 30 steps. The best settings lie on a ridge of roughly constant step size times number of steps, and settings beyond it diverge. Tuned for a related article the best setting is $3 \times 10^{-5}$ with 3 steps, and tuned for the same document $3 \times 10^{-5}$ with 10 steps. A coarser first search with plain gradient descent did not bracket its optimum.

Tuned for a related article, dynamic evaluation lowers perplexity by 0.224 on the same document and by 0.029 after a related article, and raises it by 0.019 after an unrelated article and by 0.022 after another source. Tuned for the same document, it lowers perplexity on the document's own text by 0.271 but raises it by 0.236 after a related article and by 0.284 after an unrelated one.
