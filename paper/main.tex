This study asks whether the fast weights that test-time training (TTT) builds while a language model reads one document can be carried into the next document, how much that carry contributes, and which domains benefit most. The data are the SlimPajama-6B corpus, which mixes six sources (ArXiv, Book, C4, Github, StackExchange and Wikipedia), and English Wikipedia articles from the November 2023 dump. Models are trained on 4000 SlimPajama documents and evaluated on 106 held-out documents, on 200 Wikipedia target articles, each read after text of varying relatedness, and on a confirmatory test set of 200 further targets and 73 SlimPajama documents whose analysis plan was published before it was run.

The problems addressed are that existing TTT methods discard their adaptation at every document boundary, that it is unknown whether a carried fast weight helps or harms the next document, and that small perplexity effects are easy to overstate. In-Place TTT layers are added to the MLP down-projections of Qwen3-0.6B and trained with a Hebbian and a chunked delta update rule, against a LoRA-only model trained with the same data, schedule and learning rate. Paired evaluation conditions separate the training gain, adaptation within a document and the carried state, with bootstrap intervals over documents. A redesigned carry adds a separate gated path with a zero-initialised scale and is trained through the boundary between slices of one document.

Carrying the original fast weight across documents has no measurable effect, and its read-time norm clip is saturated on every measurement. The redesigned carry lowers perplexity on later slices of the same document by about 0.1, roughly 1 percent. Carried into another document, its effect is ordered by relatedness, and this replicates on the confirmatory test set at two training seeds. A carry from an article that the target mentions lowers perplexity by 0.086, about 0.5 percent, one from an unrelated article by 0.036 to 0.055, and one from another source raises it by 0.059 to 0.084. Most of this gain is generic, since a carry averaged over 200 articles lowers perplexity by about 0.07, but a related article still beats it by 0.015 and 0.018, with Bonferroni-adjusted intervals that exclude zero. In a single-seed probe trained across document boundaries, the harm from other-source text disappears, but so does the advantage of related text, and the gain within a document falls by about 40 percent.

A trained fast-weight carry therefore transfers across documents according to their relatedness and harms after a change of source. The evidence is perplexity only, so it concerns stream and domain adaptation rather than recall of what was read, and attention on the earlier text remains a far stronger channel when that text is available.

\chapter{INTRODUCTION}
\label{ch-intro}

Large language models adapt to their input only through attention, so what they learn from a document is lost once that document leaves the context. Test-time training (TTT) lets a model update part of its weights while it reads, and In-Place TTT does so in the MLP of an existing transformer, but it resets the adapted weights at every document boundary. This report studies what happens when that reset is removed and the adapted fast weight is carried into the next document. The research question is whether such a carry contributes to language modelling, when it helps and when it harms, and which domains benefit. The objectives are to build and train a session-persistent TTT model, to measure the carry against a reset and against a matched baseline, and to test any effect on a confirmatory test set fixed in advance.

As in the proposal, the study is scoped to inference-time improvement measured by perplexity on held-out text. It makes no claim that a model retains and can later use specific information, which would require behavioural testing. In the terms of \citet{song2026beyond}, our evidence is evidence of stream and domain adaptation, measured by perplexity. It does not show that a carried state stores information that the model can later retrieve. The proposal also planned individual RULER tasks \citep{hsieh2024ruler} as an additional held-out probe. They were left out. While the original designs carried nothing measurable across documents there was no carried signal for a retrieval probe to detect, and once the redesigned carry produced one, the remaining compute budget went to the relatedness, ablation, seed and dynamic-evaluation experiments that measure it in perplexity. Section~\ref{sec-limits} returns to this.

\section{Background of the study}

A trained large language model answers every input with the same parameters. This makes inference efficient, but it also means the model cannot specialise to the context in which it is being used. In-context learning lets a model adapt to its input without changing any weight, but only while that input stays in its context window \citep{brown2020language,dong2024survey}. Test-time training (TTT) relaxes this restriction by updating part of the model on the input it is currently reading, using a self-supervised objective that needs no labels \citep{sun2020ttt}. Applied to language models, TTT has been used to build sequence layers whose hidden state is itself a small model trained online \citep{sun2025learning}, long-term memory modules placed beside attention \citep{behrouz2025titans}, and most recently In-Place TTT \citep{feng2026inplace}, which turns the MLP down-projection of an existing transformer into a fast weight that adapts while the model reads. These methods descend from fast weights, weights that change during inference and act as an associative memory of what has been read \citep{ba2016using,schlag2021linear}.

\section{Justification of the problem}

Every one of these methods resets its adapted state at the end of each document. In-Place TTT does so deliberately, to prevent information leaking between unrelated inputs, and never tests carrying the fast weights forward \citep{feng2026inplace}. Related work approaches persistence from other directions. Some methods meta-learn a test-time initialisation or update weights over one long context \citep{tandon2025endtoend,bansal2025letsnot,wang2026learning}, and others carry cached activations or memory tokens across segments of a single stream \citep{dai2019transformerxl,rae2019compressive,bulatov2022rmt}. Dynamic evaluation adapts a model along its test sequence, and has been applied to successive texts, but by gradient steps on the slow weights \citep{krause2018dynamic,lazaridou2021mind}, and test-time training on retrieved neighbours of an input helps but is reset after every input \citep{hardt2024test}. Carrying a state across packed documents is not new in itself. Selective state-space models pass or reset their state at document boundaries \citep{gu2024mamba}, and in pre-training, earlier documents in the attention context help when they are related and distract when they are not \citep{zhao2024analysing}. To our knowledge, no work gives a TTT fast weight a trained carry path and measures how its transfer to the next document depends on how the two documents relate. Whether the In-Place TTT reset is necessary, and what a carried fast weight does to the next document, is the question this report studies.

\section{Objectives of the study}

This report addresses two research questions, as stated in the research proposal.
\begin{description}
  \item[Q1.] How much does carry, the information retained from the previous session, contribute to language model improvement?
  \item[Q2.] What kind of domains perform best with session-persistent TTT?
\end{description}

The primary objective is to develop and empirically analyse a session-persistent test-time training method for pretrained language models. Following the proposal, the method exposes two logically independent components that can be measured separately, within-document adaptation without any persistence, and cross-document persistence of the fast weight.

The study makes four contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item An analysis of the naive carry. With the In-Place TTT reset removed, the carried fast weight contributes nothing measurable to the next document, at three training checkpoints and for a Hebbian and a delta update rule. The read-time clip is saturated on every measurement, so a carried state reaches the output only as a direction, but the redesigned carry works with the clip still saturated, so the clip alone does not explain the null result (Sections~\ref{ch-q1} and~\ref{ch-why}).
  \item A carry design trained within documents. A separate, zero-initialised carry path, trained through the boundary between slices, raises the gain from carrying state between slices of a document about sevenfold at step 200, and replicates with a second training seed. A single-seed ablation suggests that its two main changes work together (Section~\ref{ch-design}).
  \item A relatedness gradient for transfer across documents, replicated on a preregistered confirmatory test set. The redesigned carry helps the next document most when it is the same article, less when it is a related article and less again when it is an unrelated article of the same source, and it harms after text from another source. Most of the gain is a generic effect of the source, but a related article beats a generic carry in both seeds. In a single-seed probe, training across document boundaries removes the harm and with it the document-specific part (Section~\ref{ch-design}).
  \item A comparison with attention and with repeated reading. Attention on the preceding text of the same document gives a gain about 25 times the carry's. The carry's state is as large as the attention cache of about 1500 tokens, and it gains nothing from reading the same text again (Section~\ref{ch-design}).
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{LITERATURE REVIEW}
\label{ch-lit}

This chapter reviews test-time training (Section~\ref{sec-lit-ttt}), fast weights and the update rules, gates and stabilisers that govern them (Section~\ref{sec-lit-fw}), how earlier models keep information across segments and documents (Section~\ref{sec-lit-persist}), dynamic evaluation and continual learning (Section~\ref{sec-lit-dyneval}) and evaluation methodology (Section~\ref{sec-lit-eval}). It closes by positioning this study (Section~\ref{sec-lit-gap}).

\section{Test-time training}
\label{sec-lit-ttt}

Test-time training was introduced by \citet{sun2020ttt} for image classification under distribution shift. A shared feature extractor takes a few gradient steps on a self-supervised loss of the single test input, the prediction is made with the updated extractor, and the update is discarded before the next input. The authors give a sufficient condition for this to help in the convex case, namely that the gradients of the self-supervised and main losses are positively correlated. An online variant starts each input from the extractor adapted on the previous one, but assumes a stream from one fixed or slowly changing distribution. Later work in vision kept the reset. \citet{gandelsman2022ttt} discard the adapted weights after every input and show that a misaligned self-supervised signal can lower accuracy. Continual test-time adaptation drops the reset entirely. In the formulation surveyed by \citet{maharana2026continual}, a model is updated online over a sequence of target distributions and never reset, the two failure modes are forgetting and the accumulation of errors from noisy self-generated targets, and stochastic restoration of part of the weights bounds long-horizon drift. Persistence is therefore possible, but it has to be bounded.

In language models, \citet{sun2025learning} make the hidden state of a sequence layer itself a model, updated by a gradient step on a self-supervised loss for each token or mini-batch, and each sequence starts again from the learned initial state. Titans \citep{behrouz2025titans} train a neural memory online with momentum and an adaptive weight decay that acts as a forget gate, within a single context per example. \citet{tandon2025endtoend} meta-learn the initial state of fast weights in the MLPs of the last quarter of the blocks, and discard training documents shorter than 8K tokens specifically to avoid resetting at packed document boundaries. On needle-in-a-haystack retrieval, full attention outperforms every test-time training method they test. Query-only TTT \citep{bansal2025letsnot} updates the query projections within one prompt, and \citet{akyurek2025surprising} train a LoRA adapter per task and discard it, finding that a shared adapter helps on some task families and interferes on others.

In-Place TTT \citep{feng2026inplace} is the starting point of this report. It repurposes the down-projection of a gated MLP as the fast weight, so it can be added to a pretrained model without a new layer. For each chunk it applies the current fast weight and then updates it with an outer product of a target and the MLP activation, a Hebbian accumulation with no error term. The target is a causal depthwise convolution of the input followed by a learned projection, aligned with next-token prediction. For long sequences, each chunk's update is clipped by its Frobenius norm for stability. In-Place TTT improves the RULER accuracy of Qwen3-4B at long context, and at document boundaries the fast weights are reset to prevent leakage between independent sequences. No partial carry or decay is tested. \citet{wang2026learning} replace the target with test-time context distillation, an error-correcting write, and \citet{ouyang2026ttt} use the layer's own next-position hidden state as the target. Under matched continual pretraining they find that the original target does not improve RULER over the backbone, while theirs does, for four models including Qwen3-0.6B. Both stay within one input, and \citet{ouyang2026ttt} restore the weights after every sample.

Test-time training differs from in-context learning, which adapts a frozen model through its context alone and is bounded by the context window \citep{brown2020language,dong2024survey}. In the evaluation of this report each slice is read without the previous text in attention, so the carried state is the only channel between slices, and a gain from carrying it cannot come from in-context learning over earlier text.

\section{Fast weights, update rules and stability}
\label{sec-lit-fw}

\citet{ba2016using} revive fast weights in recurrent networks with a decayed outer product of the hidden state, $A(t) = \lambda A(t-1) + \eta\, h(t) h(t)^\top$, and need layer normalisation to keep it stable. Causal linear attention is a recurrent network whose state adds the outer product of each key and value \citep{katharopoulos2020transformers}, and \citet{schlag2021linear} identify this state as a fast-weight matrix. The fast weight of In-Place TTT belongs to this family. Read as an associative memory, such a state has limited capacity. An additive matrix memory holds at most as many associations as its key dimension without retrieval error \citep{schlag2021linear}, and under the Hebbian rule the number of storable associations is bounded by the rank of the state \citep{mcdermott2026lifelong}.

The update rule decides how the state behaves when it keeps being written. The additive, or Hebbian, rule adds every outer product in full and cannot overwrite a stale association. The delta rule writes the difference between the new value and the one currently retrieved, and lowers WikiText-103 perplexity by 1.5 to 2.8 points at matched size \citep{schlag2021linear}. \citet{yang2024deltanet} derive it as one step of online gradient descent on a regression loss, so the update scales with the prediction error, and with normalised keys its state cannot grow without bound. Error correction is not uniformly beneficial. Fine-tuning GPT-2 into fast-weight models, \citet{mao2022finetuning} finds the additive rule slightly ahead of the delta rule under the best configuration, and the delta rule diverging unless its features are normalised and its gate starts very small. \citet{yang2025gated} find decay and the delta rule complementary.

A gate or a decay lets a state forget. In a controlled ablation, linear attention with no gate reaches a training perplexity of 23.21, against 16.55 with a fixed decay and 14.77 with a fine-grained gate \citep{yang2024gla}. Mamba makes its state update depend on the input, and notes that when documents are packed together a time-invariant model bleeds information from one sequence into the next, whereas a selective model can learn to reset its state \citep{gu2024mamba}. The mLSTM writes its matrix memory with learned forget and input gates \citep{beck2024xlstm}, and the RWKV authors note that funnelling all past context through one fixed-size state limits the recall of fine detail \citep{peng2023rwkv}.

Every family needs a mechanism that keeps its state in a usable range. Linear attention divides its read by an accumulated normaliser \citep{katharopoulos2020transformers}, recurrent fast weights use layer normalisation \citep{ba2016using}, TTT layers wrap their inner model in normalisation and a residual \citep{sun2025learning}, and In-Place TTT clips each update \citep{feng2026inplace}. Large-chunk TTT normalises the fast weights after every update against magnitude explosion \citep{zhang2025test}, and Generative Adapter resets all singular values of its outer-product update to one \citep{chen2024generative}. A decay or a pull-back keeps the relative scale of recent writes, while a norm bound applied to a large state keeps only its direction, which is the property Section~\ref{ch-why} examines. Large-chunk TTT and Generative Adapter keep only direction and still work, so discarding magnitude does not by itself make a fast weight useless.

\section{Persistence across segments and documents}
\label{sec-lit-persist}

Several architectures keep information beyond the current segment. Transformer-XL \citep{dai2019transformerxl} caches the previous segment's hidden states for the next segment to attend to, so what persists is a cache of activations and no parameter is updated. The Compressive Transformer \citep{rae2019compressive} compresses old activations into a second memory and is trained without resetting it at article boundaries. The Recurrent Memory Transformer \citep{bulatov2022rmt} passes memory tokens between segments and trains them by backpropagation through several segments, and deeper unrolling is what makes the memory useful. Memorizing Transformers \citep{wu2022memorizing} retrieve past keys and values by nearest-neighbour search and clear this memory at the start of each new document.

Other methods compress earlier text into a compact state that a later input can use. AutoCompressors \citep{chevalier2023adapting} turn each segment into summary vectors read as soft prompts, trained with gradients stopped after two compression steps, and although they are trained within documents, the vectors of separately retrieved documents can be combined and still help. Infini-attention \citep{munkhdalai2024leave} keeps a compressive associative memory across the segments of one input. MemoryLLM \citep{wang2024memoryllm} keeps a pool of memory tokens in every layer, updated by forward passes over a stream of texts with designed forgetting, and Generative Adapter \citep{chen2024generative} maps a stream of context chunks to a low-rank weight update in a single forward pass. Where such methods are compared with keeping the full text in context, the text often remains more accurate. On multi-session conversations, prompting with the full history reaches an F1 of 66.0 against 40.2 for Generative Adapter.

Persistence of fast weights is rarer. \citet{schlag2021linear} carry the fast-weight state across the training segments of WikiText-103. With the additive rule this breaks, reaching a perplexity above 1600, while the delta rule carries stably. \citet{irie2022modern} find that resetting self-modifying weights after every fixed span removes their benefit. In test-time training the reset is the rule. Per-sample resets are a stated design choice in vision \citep{sun2020ttt,gandelsman2022ttt}, TTT layers reset per sequence \citep{sun2025learning}, In-Place TTT and end-to-end TTT reset at document boundaries \citep{feng2026inplace,tandon2025endtoend}, and Titans and context distillation stay within one context \citep{behrouz2025titans,wang2026learning}. \citet{tiezzi2025state} note that learning online from an unsegmented stream without resets remains largely unexplored for recurrent models. \citet{kim2026delayed} argue that next-token training gives a test-time memory little reason to keep information through many later updates, and add a delayed loss whose gradient reaches the earlier writes.

\section{Dynamic evaluation and continual learning}
\label{sec-lit-dyneval}

Dynamic evaluation \citep{krause2018dynamic} adapts a model's parameters by gradient descent on each segment of the test sequence as it is read. Its best update rescales gradients with training statistics and decays the parameters back towards their trained values, and a sparse variant that adapts a small matrix recovers most of the gain. The benefit rises and then levels off within a few thousand characters of in-distribution text. \citet{lazaridou2021mind} apply it to temporal drift. It narrows the gap to a model trained on newer data, but forgets older data as it adapts. Their analysis of new words also finds that a second occurrence becomes much easier only while the first is still in the attention cache. Dynamic evaluation therefore already carries adaptation from earlier text, but by gradient steps on the slow weights rather than by a fast weight built in the forward pass.

A state carried from one document to the next is a form of continual learning and inherits its central problem, catastrophic forgetting in shared distributed weights, the stability and plasticity dilemma \citep{french1999catastrophic}. In large language models, forgetting during sequential fine-tuning grows with model size \citep{li2024revisiting}. \citet{oneill2026continually} write invented facts one at a time into a model. After twenty writes it answers between 1 and 46 percent of questions about earlier facts, depending mostly on how the later facts were written, and an earlier fact's raised log-probability can remain after the model no longer answers questions about it. Surveys of continual learning for language models organise the field by training-time updates \citep{shi2024continual,zheng2024towards} and do not treat test-time parameter adaptation.

\section{Evaluation methodology}
\label{sec-lit-eval}

Perplexity on held-out text is the standard measure in this literature \citep{krause2018dynamic,lazaridou2021mind}, but it has known limits. \citet{meister2021beyond} warn that with large samples almost every test is significant even for trivial effects. Long-context benchmarks such as RULER \citep{hsieh2024ruler} and LongBench \citep{bai2023longbench} probe the use of context more directly. \citet{song2026beyond} argue that perplexity answers a narrower question than claims of memory. They separate stream or domain adaptation, measured by future-token loss, from bridge evidence that information is stored in model state and from behaviour after the original context is removed, and call the use of lower-level evidence for a higher-level claim evidence migration. They place In-Place TTT and related layers at the first level. In a controlled test, one-step updates lowered the loss on injected facts at three model scales while free-form recall stayed at zero. They recommend matched baselines for such claims, including a model without updates and the same text given in the prompt.

Three lessons shape the protocol of this report. A gain must be attributed to the mechanism that produces it, as the fragmentation ablation of Transformer-XL shows \citep{dai2019transformerxl}, so the evaluation separates training gain, within-document gain and carried gain in paired conditions. Effects in this area are small, so intervals and win rates are reported rather than significance alone \citep{meister2021beyond}. And persistence must be tested at the boundaries where it is supposed to act, which earlier methods either avoid by clearing their memory per document \citep{wu2022memorizing} or observe only in passing.

\label{sec-lit-gap}

Three patterns emerge. First, every test-time training method for language models resets its adapted state at document boundaries or never leaves a single context. Where persistence across inputs has been studied, it either assumes a homogeneous stream, carries activations rather than a fast weight, or carries slow weights updated by gradient steps.

Second, the update-rule literature predicts that a carried fast weight will be hard to keep useful. A purely additive state accumulates interference and cannot overwrite \citep{schlag2021linear,yang2024deltanet}, an ungated one cannot forget \citep{yang2024gla,mao2022finetuning}, and an adapted model that is never reset accumulates errors \citep{maharana2026continual}. The designs that work add decay, gating, error correction or a training signal that crosses the boundary \citep{yang2025gated,bulatov2022rmt,chevalier2023adapting,kim2026delayed}.

Third, whether adaptation on one text helps another depends on how the two relate. Test-time training provably helps in the convex case when its gradient is aligned with that of the main task \citep{sun2020ttt} and can hurt when its signal is misaligned \citep{gandelsman2022ttt}. Fine-tuning on retrieved neighbours of an input improves language modelling, and the order from nearest to farthest neighbour matters \citep{hardt2024test}. In pre-training on SlimPajama, packing related documents together lowers perplexity, packing documents of one source helps less, and unrelated earlier documents distract the model \citep{zhao2024analysing}. That transfer follows relatedness is therefore expected, and the open question is whether a carried fast weight shows it.

The gap is therefore specific. To our knowledge, no prior work gives a TTT fast weight a trained carry path, measures what the carry contributes against a reset at the document boundary, or asks how its effect depends on the relatedness of the earlier text. This report adds such a carry to an In-Place TTT design, compares a Hebbian and a delta update rule, and measures within-document and cross-document effects separately with paired conditions and bootstrap intervals.

The study is experimental. Pretrained language models are extended with fast-weight layers, trained under matched settings, and compared on held-out text in paired evaluation conditions that isolate each source of gain. \section{Data Preprocessing}

Training uses 4000 documents of at least 2048 tokens, truncated to 16384 tokens and balanced across sources towards a target mix of 25 percent C4, 20 percent each of Github and ArXiv, 15 percent Book, and 10 percent each of Wikipedia and StackExchange. Except in one probe (Section~\ref{sec-mixed}), each session is one document, read whole if shorter than 2100 tokens and otherwise split into two to six slices of at least 1000 tokens.

The held-out set is drawn from documents never used in training. It is drawn from the last 15{,}000 rows of the dataset, which are removed before any training stage, and long enough to cut into slices. Every model is evaluated on exactly the same documents.

Wikipedia articles of at least 6000 characters serve as primes and those of at least 12{,}000 characters as targets. Each article is tokenised with the model's tokenizer and cut into blocks of 1024 tokens, the target's second block is the text that is scored, and the primes are 1024-token blocks chosen as described in Section~\ref{sec-relatedness-method}. For the confirmatory test set, articles whose opening text shares at least five ten-word sequences with any Wikipedia document in the training data are removed, and documents of the development holdout are removed from the confirmatory holdout.

\section{Models and training}

All models start from the pretrained Qwen3-0.6B model. TTT layers replace the MLPs of the 14 odd-numbered layers, 1, 3 and so on up to 27.

\subsection{In-Place TTT on the MLP down-projection}

Every transformer block applies a position-wise feed-forward MLP after attention \citep{vaswani2017attention}, and In-Place TTT \citep{feng2026inplace} uses the final, down-projection matrix of this MLP as its fast weight. Let $z \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{ff}}$ be the gated activation of a transformer MLP and $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d_{ff}}$ its down-projection. In-Place TTT adds a fast weight $S$ of the same shape, built during the forward pass, and a learned scalar gate $g(h) = \sigma(w_g^\top h + b_g)$ computed from the layer input $h$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{out} = W_0 z + g(h)\, \eta\, S z .
  \label{eq-out}
\end{equation}
The fast weight is written in chunks of $C$ tokens. For chunk $k$ the layer forms a target $V_k$ from a causal depthwise convolution of the normalised hidden states followed by a learned projection $W_{\mathrm{target}}$. $W_{\mathrm{target}}$ is zero-initialised, so every TTT layer is identical to the base MLP at the start of training. Chunk $k$ reads only the state accumulated from strictly earlier chunks, which keeps the layer causal. Figure~\ref{fig-arch} shows the layer.

\subsection{Update rules}

\paragraph{Hebbian.} The Hebbian rule, the form used by In-Place TTT and by linear attention \citep{feng2026inplace,katharopoulos2020transformers}, writes an outer product with no error term,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta S_k = \tfrac{1}{C} V_k^\top Z_k, \qquad S_k = S_{\mathrm{carried}} + \textstyle\sum_{j<k} \Delta S_j .
\end{equation}
Nothing in this write checks whether $S$ already predicts $V$, so the state grows with every chunk.

\paragraph{Chunked delta.} The chunked delta rule writes the residual of the current prediction, with the state frozen at the start of each chunk,
\begin{equation}
  \hat V_k = \frac{\eta}{1 + \lVert z \rVert^2}\, Z_k S_k^\top, \qquad \Delta S_k = \tfrac{1}{C} (V_k - \hat V_k)^\top Z_k ,
\end{equation}
with the normalised step $\eta / (1 + \lVert z \rVert^2)$ applied per token row. For this rule the read in Equation~\ref{eq-out} uses the same normalised step, so the TTT term is $\hat V_k$. The delta rule was introduced for fast weights by \citet{schlag2021linear} and amounts to a step of online gradient descent on a regression loss \citep{yang2024deltanet}. This is the mini-batch form of the delta rule used by \citet{sun2025learning}, and its error term lets a state that already predicts the target stop growing.

\subsection{Session persistence}

A session is a sequence of work items, either whole documents or slices of one document. At the end of each item the layer folds the item's total update into a carried state with an exponential moving average,
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{carried}} \leftarrow \lambda\, S_{\mathrm{carried}} + \Delta S_{\mathrm{item}}, \qquad \lambda = 0.9 ,
\end{equation}
and the next item starts from $S_{\mathrm{carried}}$ instead of from zero. This is the decayed accumulation that \citet{ba2016using} use for fast weights within a sequence, applied here between items. In the original design the carried state is detached between items, which limits backpropagation through time to one item, and the redesign of Section~\ref{ch-design} keeps it in the graph for two items. During training every session is a single document, except in the probe of Section~\ref{sec-mixed}, so the carry is learned only between the slices of one document. Carrying it across documents is applied at evaluation, as a test of whether a state learned within documents transfers to the next one. Figure~\ref{fig-persist} contrasts this with resetting.

\subsection{Stabilisation}

Two mechanisms keep the fast weights stable. A Frobenius norm clip rescales the state whenever it is read, so that $\lVert \eta S \rVert_F \le \tau$ with $\tau = 20$, while preserving its direction. In-Place TTT similarly bounds the Frobenius norm of each chunk's update \citep{feng2026inplace}, while here the bound is applied to the state when it is read. For the Hebbian rule the clip acts only on the read, while for the delta rule the clipped state is also what the next chunk accumulates onto. The output gate is initialised with bias $b_g = -2$, so the TTT term enters at about 12 percent strength at the start of training and the model can learn to open or close it. Separately, the gradients of all trainable parameters are clipped to a total norm of 10. The read-time clip bounds the forward pass, including at evaluation where no gradients exist, while the gradient clip bounds each optimiser step. Section~\ref{ch-why} returns to the role of the read-time clip.

\subsection{Training objective and parameters}

All models are trained with the standard next-token cross-entropy. LoRA adapters \citep{hu2022lora} are trained in every model, so that the TTT models and the control differ only in the TTT layers. The TTT models additionally train the down-projection $W_0$ of the TTT layers and the new modules (target projection, convolution and gate). Counted from the layer shapes of Qwen3-0.6B, this gives about 68.0 million trainable parameters for a TTT model (9.2 million LoRA, 44.0 million in the 14 down-projections, 14.8 million in the new modules) against about 10.1 million for the LoRA-only control. This difference matters for interpreting the results and is discussed in Sections~\ref{ch-q1} and~\ref{sec-limits}.

All runs use LoRA of rank 16 with $\alpha = 32$ and dropout 0.05, a LoRA learning rate of $10^{-5}$, AdamW, 16 gradient accumulation steps, a warmup of 2 percent of steps, cosine decay over the full epoch of 1050 optimiser steps, and seed 42. TTT runs train their down-projections at $3 \times 10^{-5}$ and their new modules at $6 \times 10^{-2}$. Checkpoints at steps 200, 400 and 600 are evaluated. Because the epoch length depends only on the data and the session strategy, all seed-42 runs share the same 1050-step schedule and data order. The second seed of the carry design selects different documents, giving 1048 steps, and the probe of Section~\ref{sec-mixed} keeps the seed-42 documents and step count but groups them into different sessions. Appendix~\ref{app-hparams} lists every setting.

  \item[LoRA-only.] The matched control, with no TTT layers.
  \item[Hebbian.] TTT layers with the Hebbian rule.
  \item[Delta.] TTT layers with the chunked delta rule.
\end{description}

\subsection{The redesigned carry}
\label{sec-design-method}

The results in Sections~\ref{ch-q1} and~\ref{ch-why} show that the carry, as first designed, contributes little even between slices of the same document, where the text it carries is directly relevant. Three features of the training setup are plausible causes. The carried state was detached between slices, so only the parts of the model that read the carry were trained, never the parts that decide what to write. The carry was added into the same sum as the current slice's state and clipped together with it, so the current slice could dominate it. And the Hebbian rule starts training with a loss spike during which the fast weights are unusable. The saturated clip of Section~\ref{ch-why} is kept, so the redesign also tests whether a carry whose magnitude never reaches the output can still be useful once its write is trained. A redesigned carry, built for the Hebbian rule and trained and evaluated with the same protocol, addresses these three points.

All changes are configuration options, and every other setting is identical to the original Hebbian run.

\paragraph{A separate carry path.} The carried state is read through its own term with its own clip, gate and scale,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{out} = W_0 z + g(h)\, \eta\, S_{\mathrm{in}} z + \alpha\, g_c(h)\, \eta\, \mathrm{clip}(S_{\mathrm{carried}})\, z ,
\end{equation}
where $S_{\mathrm{in}}$ is the state built inside the current slice, $g_c$ is a second gate and $\alpha$ is a learnable scalar initialised to zero. As with the zero-initialised factor of LoRA \citep{hu2022lora}, the layer is therefore exactly the original Hebbian layer at initialisation, and the carried term enters only if training finds it useful.

\paragraph{Training through the carry.} The carried state is kept in the autograd graph between the slices of a session, so the loss on a later slice also trains what an earlier slice wrote. This follows the Recurrent Memory Transformer, whose memory improves substantially when it is trained by backpropagation across more segments \citep{bulatov2022rmt}. To bound memory, the carry is detached after every second slice and at every optimiser step. AutoCompressors make the same choice, stopping gradients after two compression steps with no loss of quality \citep{chevalier2023adapting}. Gradient checkpointing re-runs each layer's forward pass during the backward pass, and with the carry in the graph a layer can be re-run after its carry has moved on, which silently corrupts the gradient. The recompute is therefore given the carry the layer actually read, and a test confirms that gradients with and without checkpointing are identical, and that they differ without this correction.

\paragraph{A nearly closed main gate at the start.} The main gate's bias is initialised at $-6$ instead of $-2$, so the in-document fast weights start at about 0.25 percent strength rather than 12 percent. This is close to where the original Hebbian model's gate ends after training (0.002).

\subsection{Evaluation conditions}

Each document is cut into 8 equal slices and measured in four conditions (Table~\ref{tab-conditions}).

Differences between conditions give the decomposition reported throughout.
\begin{description}
  \item[Training gain.] Base minus TTT off. Everything the slow weights learned, including LoRA and, for TTT models, the trained $W_0$.
  \item[Within-document gain.] TTT off minus TTT reset per slice. What the fast weights learn inside a slice.
  \item[Between-slice gain.] TTT reset per slice minus TTT carried. What carrying state from earlier slices of the same document adds.
  \item[Matched gain.] The LoRA-only model minus the TTT model with its fast weights carried. The total advantage over the control.
\end{description}

\subsection{Persistence suites}

Three further suites probe persistence directly.
\begin{description}
  \item[Boundary.] For each source, a chain of five documents is read under three conditions, carrying state across documents, resetting at document boundaries, and resetting at every slice. The cross-document gain compares the first two conditions on documents two to five of each chain (24 documents), and the cross-slice gain compares the last two on all 30 documents. The condition that resets at document boundaries follows the reset rule of In-Place TTT, so the cross-document gain compares our persistent method with a reset in the style of In-Place TTT on the same model, data and documents. It is not a reproduction of the original system, which uses chunks of 512 or 1024 tokens, clips each chunk's update rather than the state, has no output gate and no LoRA, and was evaluated on models of 4B parameters and more \citep{feng2026inplace}.
  \item[Repeat.] Each document is read ten times in a row with its carried state never reset. Each later reading is compared with the first, and the tenth reading is also compared with the second, which tests whether the carry keeps compounding after one exposure. This is the most favourable test possible for a carried state, since the state was built from the very document being read again.
  \item[Swap.] A target document is read after the state has been primed on two documents from the same source or from a different source, each read whole, and compared with reading it unprimed, to test whether any carried benefit is source-specific, as planned in the proposal.
\end{description}

\subsection{Relatedness suite}
\label{sec-relatedness-method}

The boundary suite compares two points, the same document and a different document of the same source. To test documents that are distinct but related, a further suite uses English Wikipedia, a source on which the redesigned carry gains strongly between slices (Section~\ref{ch-q2}). The articles come from two shards of the November 2023 English dump. They may overlap the Wikipedia portion of the training data. Every condition scores the same target tokens, so an overlap affects the target alike in every condition, although a prime seen in training may act differently from an unseen one. For each of 200 target articles, chosen in a seeded order, the second block of 1024 tokens is scored after the carry is reset and after it is primed by a block of 1024 tokens from one of four texts, ordered by relatedness.
\begin{description}
  \item[Same document.] The target article's own first 1024 tokens.
  \item[Related article.] The article the target mentions most often by title, at least twice, matching whole multi-word titles only.
  \item[Random article.] An article that neither mentions the target nor is mentioned by it.
  \item[Other source.] A held-out document from one of the other five SlimPajama sources.
\end{description}
Every prime has the same length and every condition scores exactly the same target tokens, so only the prime varies. Because relatedness is defined by title mentions, a related prime also tends to share more words with the target, and no control here separates relatedness in topic from word overlap. The same 200 cases, identical title for title, were measured on the carry design and on the original Hebbian model, both at step 600. These are development cases, and Section~\ref{sec-confirmatory} repeats the suite on confirmatory ones.

\subsection{Confirmatory test set fixed in advance}
\label{sec-confirmatory-method}

The development results come from the documents on which the design was developed. To test the design on data it was not tuned on, the relatedness suite was rerun on new cases with an analysis plan published before the run (a preregistration), which fixes the cases, the conditions, the contrasts and the rules that decide which claims may be made. The 200 cases come from two other shards of the same Wikipedia dump, with a different case seed, each related article used for one target only. A case is skipped if its target or primes share at least five ten-word sequences with any Wikipedia document in either seed's training data, which removed one case. Three conditions are added. A generic carry is the mean of the carried states of 200 further articles, rescaled in each layer to the median norm of a single carried state, and tests whether the gain after a related article is more than a generic effect of reading Wikipedia. An in-context condition reads the related prime and the target as one input with the carry reset, which is also the equal-memory baseline, since the prime is shorter than the 1500 tokens the carried state could hold as an attention cache. A chain condition carries 1, 2, 5 or 10 random articles in sequence before the target. Intervals use 10{,}000 bootstrap resamples over cases, and the four primary contrasts also get Bonferroni-adjusted 98.75 percent intervals. The other-source primes are drawn from 62 SlimPajama documents and so recur across cases, which the case-level bootstrap does not account for, so the intervals for that condition are somewhat too narrow. The main evaluation of Table~\ref{tab-conditions} was rerun on a new holdout draw from which the 106 development documents were removed. The holdout pool is small, so this left 73 documents from five sources and none from Book, a shortfall the plan did not anticipate. These results are therefore reported pooled over sources only.

\subsection{Probe trained across documents}
\label{sec-mixed-method}

The main models are trained only within documents, so their use across documents is a transfer test. To see what changes when the carry is trained across boundaries, a single-seed probe was trained with the same data, seed, schedule and settings as the seed-42 carry design, except that each training session chains five documents, each next document from the same source with probability one half and otherwise from another source, with the carried state never reset inside a session. It was evaluated on the same confirmatory cases, and each contrast is its gain over its own reset carry minus that of the seed-42 model, both resampled on the same cases.

\subsection{Statistics}

Perplexity within a document is token-weighted over its slices. A gap between two conditions is the difference of their geometric-mean perplexities over documents, positive when the second condition has lower perplexity. We report 95 percent percentile bootstrap intervals from 1000 resamples of documents (10{,}000 for the confirmatory test set of Section~\ref{sec-confirmatory}), with both conditions resampled on the same indices, and the win rate, the fraction of documents on which the second condition is lower. A star marks an interval that excludes zero. Following \citet{meister2021beyond}, every result is reported as an effect size with its interval rather than as a significance test alone.

\subsection{Measurement integrity}

Two errors in the evaluation pipeline were found and fixed during the study, and both are reported for transparency. First, the in-training evaluation of the original runs ran with LoRA dropout active, which perturbed every condition slightly. The fix puts every evaluation, in training and final, in evaluation mode. All numbers in Sections~\ref{ch-q1} to~\ref{ch-why} come from the final evaluation. Section~\ref{ch-design} also reports in-training evaluation points to describe training dynamics, and all of them come from runs made after the fix. Second, the base condition initially kept the trained $W_0$ of the TTT layers instead of the pretrained one, which understated the training gain of TTT models. The fixed pipeline restores the pretrained $W_0$, and earlier evaluations were corrected by taking the base condition from the LoRA-only evaluation, which contains the true base model on the same documents. Two sanity checks confirm the pipeline. The LoRA-only model, which has no fast weights, gives a within-document and between-slice gain of exactly 0.000 at every step, and the base-model perplexities are identical across all LoRA-only evaluations. Checkpoints of an earlier, differently configured run that had been saved under the same name were identified by their timestamps and excluded.

\section{Project Outcomes}

\subsection{The naive carry}
\label{ch-q1}

Switching the fast weights on inside a slice lowers perplexity at every checkpoint for both rules, on 72 to 100 percent of documents. The delta rule's within-document gain is 9 times the Hebbian gain at step 200, 4 times at step 400 and 1.7 times at step 600. It is largest early, 0.144 at step 200 and 0.276 at step 100, and settles near 0.06, which is consistent with the slow weights absorbing part of what the fast weights first provided.

The gain does not build up along a document (Figure~\ref{fig-position}). It is already present in the first slice, dips in the middle slices and shows no upward trend, so the fast weights adapt locally rather than accumulating knowledge of the document. Carrying state from earlier slices of the same document adds a small gain for the delta rule, 0.012 at step 600 and about 0.015 per slice from the second slice on. For the original designs, this is the only form of persistence found to help.

  \centering

  \caption{Gains at step 600 by slice index, with 95 percent bootstrap bands. Left, fast-weight updates within a slice. Right, state carried from earlier slices, zero at the first slice by construction.}
  \label{fig-position}
\end{figure}

The cross-document gain lies between $-0.004$ and $+0.008$ perplexity at every checkpoint for both rules, and every interval includes zero (Table~\ref{tab-main}, Figure~\ref{fig-gains} right). Three times more training does not move it. Since the reset condition of the boundary suite follows the reset rule of In-Place TTT, this is also the comparison with a reset in that style. On the same model, data and documents, the naive carry adds nothing measurable to it. These intervals come from 24 documents and are about $\pm 0.01$ wide, so they rule out an effect larger than about 0.01 rather than proving that there is none, and Section~\ref{ch-design} measures the redesigned carry on about 100 documents.

Table~\ref{tab-persist} collects the persistence measures at step 600. Re-reading a document with its own carried state never helps. Against the first reading, the gain of every later reading up to the tenth is within 0.002 perplexity, and no interval excludes zero, for either rule (Figure~\ref{fig-repeat}). The tenth reading against the second is $+0.000$ with an interval of $\pm 0.001$ for both rules, even though the carried state was built from that very document. The swap suite shows that a foreign carried state can do harm. For the Hebbian model, a state primed on a different source raises perplexity in 15 of the 30 source pairs, by 0.022 on average and by up to 0.07, and never lowers it significantly, while same-source priming is close to neutral ($-0.008$). The Hebbian same-against-other effect of 0.010 in Table~\ref{tab-persist} therefore reflects a foreign state hurting less, not a same-source state helping. For the delta model, priming is neutral in either case, with 3 of 30 pairs significant in mixed directions. For the original designs, no carried state helps the next document.

For the naive carry, information retained from the previous document contributes nothing measurable to language model improvement in this setting, where the carry is learned within documents and applied across them. The improvement of a TTT model over a matched baseline comes from extra trainable capacity and from adaptation within each document, and the only persistence that helps is carrying state between slices of the same document with the delta rule. Section~\ref{ch-design} revisits this answer with a redesigned carry, which makes the carry between slices of the same document substantially useful. Primed on a single block of earlier text, it transfers to the next document, most when the two are related and least, turning to harm, when they come from different sources, while a long accumulated history of whole documents harms at step 600.

\subsection{The saturated clip}
\label{ch-why}

\paragraph{The read-time clip is always active.} The clip binds whenever $\lVert \eta S \rVert_F > \tau$. In units of the pretrained down-projection, this is a state ratio $\lVert \eta S \rVert_F / \lVert W_0 \rVert_F$ above $\tau / \lVert W_0 \rVert_F = 0.43$, given a mean $\lVert W_0 \rVert_F$ of 46.6 over the TTT layers. Across every boundary, repeat, swap and main-evaluation measurement at steps 200, 400 and 600, the state ratio is above this threshold on 100 percent of measurements, by a factor of at least 255 for the Hebbian model and at least 1597 for the delta model (Table~\ref{tab-clip}, Figure~\ref{fig-dist}).

\paragraph{Only the direction of the state reaches the output.} A saturated clip means that the magnitude of the state never reaches the output. What reaches it is a vector of norm $\tau$ pointing along the accumulated state. Two observations follow. First, re-reading a document grows the carried state by a factor of about 6.5 over ten readings, from a median state ratio of about 14{,}600 after the first reading to about 95{,}000 after the tenth for the delta model, and from about 2{,}600 to 16{,}900 for the Hebbian model, while perplexity stays flat (Figure~\ref{fig-repeat}, which starts at the second reading). Second, carrying state into a new document makes it about two to four times larger than a state reset at the boundary, again with no effect on perplexity (Figure~\ref{fig-chain}). With $\lambda = 0.9$ the carried direction is an average over earlier documents, and a plausible reading is that, once normalised to $\tau$, it holds nothing the next document can use.

\paragraph{Removing the clip does not help.} If saturation erases the information in the state's magnitude, removing the clip might let persistence work. We trained both rules without the clip, keeping every other setting, the data order and the gradient clip (Figure~\ref{fig-noclip}).

The Hebbian model collapses. It settles at a training loss of about 8.1, a perplexity of roughly 3300, and does not recover within the 200 steps run, while the clipped Hebbian model trains normally. Its early loss, 25.8 over the first 10 steps, is not by itself evidence, because a clipped Hebbian model can also start with a loss spike that it recovers from (Section~\ref{ch-design}). The unclipped delta model, reading the same data in the same order, stays between 1.8 and 2.8 throughout. A likely account is that without an error term and with a fixed step the Hebbian state grows with every chunk until it overwhelms the forward pass, but this one short run does not isolate the cause.

\paragraph{The optimiser closed the gate when the fast weights were unusable.} The same response appears twice (Figure~\ref{fig-gates}). The clipped Hebbian model also has a nearly closed gate, with a median of at most 0.003 at every chain position, against about 0.78 for the clipped delta model, and the unclipped delta model closes its gate fully. In both cases the TTT term was too large or too noisy to use, and training reduced it to almost nothing. Two observations do not make this a rule, but they suggest that a closed gate is the optimiser's default response to unusable fast weights.

\paragraph{Summary.} In these runs the clip is what keeps the fast weights usable, by reducing an unusably large state to a fixed and usable scale, and it does so by discarding the magnitude of the state. In the naive carry, whose write is never trained to serve a later read, the direction that remains is uninformative even about the document that produced it, and in two short single-seed probes removing the clip made the fast weights either unstable or unused. The cause of the naive null result is argued from the mechanism and supported by these probes, rather than shown by a single intervention. A saturated clip does not by itself rule out a useful carry. Section~\ref{ch-design} keeps the clip, which stays saturated, and instead changes how the carry is read and trained, so that the direction it carries is learned to be useful.

\subsection{The redesigned carry}
\label{ch-design}

\paragraph{Two failed attempts.} The design of Section~\ref{sec-design-method} is the third attempt, and the first two explain its last two features.

The first attempt used the separate carry path without the zero-initialised scale and with the usual gate bias of $-2$. At step 100, carrying state between slices raised perplexity from 16.3 to 31.5, worse on every source. The reason is the size of a clipped state. The clip bounds the state at $\tau = 20$, which is about 4.2 to 5.3 times the largest singular value of the pretrained down-projection (3.8 to 4.8 in layers 1, 13 and 27). A clipped state concentrated in few directions, as outer-product states tend to be, therefore adds about half the strength of $W_0$ along those directions even through a gate at 12 percent. The zero-initialised scale removes this.

The second attempt added the scale but kept the gate bias at $-2$. This time the in-document fast weights, not the carry, broke the model during early training. With them switched on, perplexity was 4703 at step 50 and 1024 at step 100, against 16.6 and 16.2 with them off, and the training loss opened at 17.5. The carry cannot be the cause, since its scale starts at zero and the failing condition resets the state at every slice, so the spike comes from the Hebbian fast weights within a slice. The model recovered by step 150, but the spike occupied most of a short run. Closing the gate at the start removes it. The second attempt also ran out of GPU memory near step 195, because training through the carry held every slice of a long document in memory, which the window of two slices fixes.

With all three changes the training loss stays between about 1.9 and 2.6 from step 10, for both seeds. The design was trained twice with the same configuration and seed, once to step 200 with full evaluations at steps 100 and 200, and once to step 600 with full evaluations at steps 400 and 600. The two runs are not bitwise identical, and their in-training between-slice gains at step 100, 0.151 and 0.129, indicate run-to-run variation of about 0.02. From step 200 on, the boundary suite uses chains of 20 documents per source, so the cross-document measure rests on 100 documents instead of 24. Table~\ref{tab-design} compares the design with the original Hebbian model, and Figure~\ref{fig-design-curve} shows the carry gains across training.

By step 400 the effect is gone ($-0.003$), and at step 600 the carried state makes perplexity worse, by 0.020 $[-0.030, -0.013]$, on 71 percent of documents, with a significant harm on four of the six sources. \paragraph{The carry recovers about four percent of the gain of attention.} The context suite compares the carry with placing the previous slice directly in attention, scoring the same tokens. At step 600 the previous slice in attention lowers perplexity by 2.770 (25.7 percent), the carried state by 0.110, so the carry recovers 4.0 percent $[3.3, 4.7]$ of that gain, and 3.9 percent $[3.1, 4.6]$ at step 200. Whether it is useful where attention cannot reach, after the earlier text has left the context, is not tested.

The model leads the matched LoRA baseline by 0.234 $[0.188, 0.293]$ perplexity at step 600 on every document, against 0.106 for the original Hebbian model and 0.203 $[0.165, 0.249]$ for the delta rule, whose interval overlaps. About 0.11 of the lead is extra trainable capacity, since the design's training gain is 1.971 against 1.859 for the control.

\paragraph{The carry's effect falls steadily with relatedness.} For the carry design every step down the scale is significant (Table~\ref{tab-related}, Figure~\ref{fig-related}). A carry built from the same article lowers perplexity by 0.103, one built from an article the target mentions by 0.085, one built from an unrelated article by 0.051, and one built from a different source raises it by 0.067. The key contrast, a related article against an unrelated one, is $+0.034$ $[+0.024, +0.045]$, in favour of the related article on 70 percent of targets. A distinct but related document therefore helps clearly more than an unrelated one.

\paragraph{The gradient is specific to the redesigned carry.} On the same cases the original Hebbian carry has no significant gain at any level, and only a small harm from other-source text. The graded pattern, its help and its harm, appear only with the redesigned carry.

\paragraph{Robustness.} Title matching also links articles to date pages, such as an article mentioning ``August 1''. Removing the 18 targets whose related prime is a date page leaves every estimate within 0.003 of the full result, including the related against random contrast.

\paragraph{A random article still helps, which the boundary suite does not explain.} In the boundary suite a carried state from earlier documents of the same source harmed perplexity at step 600, and this holds on Wikipedia itself, by 0.017 (not significant) for seed 42 and by 0.051 $[0.016, 0.084]$ for seed 43. Here an unrelated Wikipedia article helps. The setups differ in what is carried. Here the prime is one block of 1024 tokens, the opening of a single article. The boundary suite instead carries the decayed state of a chain of whole earlier documents, each read in eight slices. One reading is that a long accumulation of unrelated content adds interference, but on the confirmatory cases of Section~\ref{sec-confirmatory} a chain of up to ten Wikipedia articles helps more than one, so accumulation alone does not explain the harm. We treat the difference as an open finding. Both suites agree that text from another source harms, and the relatedness suite shows that among single primes the most related text helps most.

\paragraph{The gradient replicates.} On the confirmatory cases the ordering is the same as on the development cases, in both seeds (Table~\ref{tab-confirmatory}, Figure~\ref{fig-confirmatory}). The same article helps most, then a related article, then a random one, and text of another source harms, and every step of the ordering has an interval that excludes zero. Related against random is $+0.031$ and $+0.050$, close to the development values of $+0.034$ and $+0.049$.

\paragraph{Part of the transfer is specific to the document.} The generic carry helps by about 0.07, more than a random article, so much of the gain after a Wikipedia article is a generic effect of having read Wikipedia. A related article still beats the generic carry, by 0.015 and 0.018, with adjusted intervals that exclude zero in both seeds (Table~\ref{tab-confirmatory-headline}). By the preregistered rule this allows the claim that some transfer is specific to the document. It is small, about 0.1 percent of perplexity, and the larger part of the gain is the generic one. What is specific is the relation between the prime and the target, since the related article is a different document, and because relatedness is defined by title mentions this part may rest partly on shared words.

\paragraph{A longer chain adds gain, not harm.} Carrying more random Wikipedia articles before the target helps slightly more, from 0.054 after one article to 0.071 after ten for seed 42, and ten against one is $+0.017$ $[+0.009, +0.025]$ and $+0.026$ $[+0.018, +0.034]$. Harm therefore does not grow with the number of earlier documents, at least for whole articles of the same source, and the harm after a chain in the boundary suite is not explained by accumulation. After ten articles the gain, 0.071 and 0.060, is close to that of the generic carry, 0.071 and 0.068. With a decay of 0.9 the carried state after many articles is close to an average over them, so a plausible reading, not tested further, is that a long chain converges to the generic carry. By the preregistered rule the difference between the two suites remains open. What the two suites share is that text of another source harms.

\paragraph{Attention on the prime is not cleanly comparable.} Placing the related article in attention lowers mean perplexity by 0.32, about four times the carry's gain, but it improves only 45 percent of targets. The carry has the lower perplexity on 57 and 58 percent of targets, and the mean difference between the two has an interval that includes zero. The per-target gains from attention, examined after the preregistered analysis, have a median of about $-0.14$ and a long upper tail reaching $+13.8$, so a related article in attention helps a few targets greatly and slightly harms most. That a related article in attention harms the typical target is surprising, and the condition differs from the others in more than the channel. It scores the target at positions 1024 to 2047 rather than from the start of the input, and the target's own fast weights are built on top of the prime. We therefore draw no conclusion from it beyond the preregistered one, that the mean difference between the carry and attention is not resolved.

\paragraph{Within documents and against the control.} On the 73 confirmatory SlimPajama documents the carry between slices gives $+0.125$ and $+0.118$, and the lead over the matched LoRA-only model is $+0.257$ and $+0.233$, of which $+0.120$ and $+0.108$ remain with the fast weights switched off, consistent with the development results.

\subsection{Training the carry across documents}
\label{sec-mixed}

\paragraph{The harm after other-source text disappears.} After text of another source the probe gains $+0.037$ where the within-document model loses $0.084$, a change of $+0.120$ $[+0.103, +0.140]$. The chain of ten articles gains $+0.041$ $[+0.028, +0.054]$ more, and the related article $+0.027$ $[+0.013, +0.041]$ more. These meet the preregistered rule for the claim that training across documents removes the harm, in a single-seed probe. The harm meant here is the harm after other-source text and after a chain on the confirmatory cases. The boundary suite, where the main models were harmed by a chain of SlimPajama documents, was not run on the probe.

\paragraph{The carry becomes largely generic.} In the probe every condition gains about the same (Table~\ref{tab-confirmatory}). Related against the generic carry is $-0.002$ $[-0.007, +0.003]$, and the same article against the generic carry $+0.002$ $[-0.003, +0.007]$, so the part of the transfer that was specific to the document is gone. Within a document the carry between slices falls from $+0.125$ to $+0.073$, a change of $-0.052$ $[-0.078, -0.032]$. Other-source text still gains less, $+0.037$, so the carry is not independent of the source, but within Wikipedia it no longer distinguishes related from unrelated text. Training across documents thus trades the harm for a largely generic carry, and loses about 40 percent of the gain the carry gives within a document. This is one seed and one mixing ratio.

\section{Comparison of Results}

\subsection{Against the matched baseline}

Both TTT models lead the LoRA-only control at every checkpoint (Table~\ref{tab-main}), the Hebbian model by 0.073 to 0.106 perplexity and the delta model by 0.191 to 0.232. Most of this lead is not test-time learning. A TTT model trains about 6.7 times as many parameters, and with its fast weights switched off its training gain already exceeds the control's. At step 600 the Hebbian model's lead of 0.106 splits into 0.069 of extra training gain, 0.034 within documents and 0.002 between slices, and the delta model's lead of 0.203 into 0.133, 0.058 and 0.012, so about two thirds is already present with the fast weights switched off in both, which points to the extra trainable capacity.

\subsection{Original and redesigned carry, and the two update rules}

The redesigned carry raises the gain between slices of a document from 0.014 and 0.002 for the original Hebbian design at steps 200 and 600 to 0.099 and 0.097, and it leads the matched baseline by 0.234 at step 600 against 0.106 for the original Hebbian model and 0.203 for the delta rule (Table~\ref{tab-design}). Within a slice the delta rule adapts more than the Hebbian rule, by 9 times at step 200 and 1.7 times at step 600. On the relatedness cases only the redesigned carry shows a gradient, while the original Hebbian carry has no significant gain at any level of relatedness (Table~\ref{tab-related}).

\subsection{Carry, attention and dynamic evaluation}

Placing the preceding slice of the same document in attention lowers perplexity by 2.770, about 25 times the carry's gain of 0.110 (Section~\ref{ch-design}). On the confirmatory relatedness cases, attention on a related article has a larger mean gain than the carry but is not cleanly comparable (Section~\ref{sec-confirmatory}). Dynamic evaluation of the LoRA-only model, tuned on development cases, gains more than the carry on the same document and less after a related or unrelated article (Appendix~\ref{app-dyneval}). It is a different model with different slow weights, so it supports no comparison claim.

The same mechanism, in the same model, helps on the continuation of the same document throughout training, transfers to a distinct but related document, and harms when the next text comes from another source or, in the boundary suite, follows a long chain of other documents. The relatedness suite turns this into a graded result, which replicates on preregistered confirmatory cases. The carry's benefit falls with relatedness, from the same document to a related article to an unrelated article of the same source, and it becomes harm for text from another source. Most of the gain after a Wikipedia article is generic, and a small part is specific to the document. A trained, gated fast-weight carry therefore lowers perplexity reliably after related text, by up to about one percent, still a little after unrelated text of the same source, and interferes with text of another source. This agrees with the swap result of Section~\ref{ch-q1}, where for the original Hebbian model a state primed on a different source made perplexity worse, and with the distraction by unrelated earlier documents that \citet{zhao2024analysing} find in attention. It also suggests that a carry would need a way to decide when to apply it, such as resetting on a change of topic, in the way a selective state-space model can learn to reset its state at a boundary \citep{gu2024mamba}.

\chapter{DISCUSSION}
\label{ch-discussion}

\section{Relation to the update-rule literature}

The delta rule's advantage over the Hebbian rule matches most of the literature, although \citet{mao2022finetuning} found the additive rule slightly ahead when fine-tuning GPT-2. An additive store saturates through interference and cannot overwrite stale associations \citep{schlag2021linear,yang2024deltanet}, and \citet{wang2026learning} find an error-correcting write better than In-Place TTT's additive one. Here the delta rule gives a within-document gain up to nine times larger, and only the Hebbian model collapses without the clip, as \citet{schlag2021linear} observe for the additive rule without normalisation. The gate closing on the Hebbian model echoes \citet{yang2024gla}, where an ungated additive accumulator trains poorly. The saturated clip also has a counterpart. Normalised reads, decays and the bounded transition of the delta rule keep a state in range as part of the update \citep{katharopoulos2020transformers,yang2024gla,yang2024deltanet}, whereas a clip applied at read time bounds it from outside and keeps only its direction.

\section{Relation to persistence and continual learning}

The naive carry has a fixed decay and no selectivity, and it receives no training signal across documents, since training sessions never cross a document boundary. Persistence mechanisms that work avoid this in different ways. The Recurrent Memory Transformer trains its memory through several segments \citep{bulatov2022rmt}, dynamic evaluation decays its updates towards the trained weights \citep{krause2018dynamic}, and gated and selective updates let a model choose what to keep \citep{gu2024mamba,yang2025gated}. The redesign adds a learned carry gate and training through the boundary between slices, but its main models, too, are never trained across documents. When a probe is trained across them, on a mix of same-source and other-source boundaries, the harm disappears but the carry stops distinguishing related from unrelated text. A plausible reading is that the probe found a carry that is harmless at both kinds of boundary more easily than one that tells them apart. Its harm after a long chain of documents in the boundary suite fits the continual-learning picture, in which a model that is never reset accumulates errors unless part of it is restored \citep{maharana2026continual}, and retention of an earlier write depends on how the later writes are made \citep{oneill2026continually}. The naive carry's null result also sits beside the finding of \citet{ouyang2026ttt} that the original In-Place TTT target does not improve RULER under matched training, which suggests that small gains from this layer depend heavily on what it writes.

\section{Related versus unrelated text}

The redesigned carry separates two questions that the original design left entangled. Whether a carried fast weight can lower perplexity on later text is answered yes, since it helps on the continuation of the same document once it is read through its own path and trained through. Whether it transfers beyond that document is answered by the relatedness gradient, which is the ordering already seen elsewhere. In pre-training, related earlier documents in attention help and unrelated ones distract \citep{zhao2024analysing}, test-time training on retrieved neighbours helps \citep{hardt2024test}, and a test-time update helps when its gradient is aligned with the main task and can hurt when it is not \citep{sun2020ttt,gandelsman2022ttt}. One reading, not tested here, is that a carry built from related text acts as an update aligned with the target and one built from another source as a misaligned one, with interference in shared weights \citep{french1999catastrophic} as a natural account of the harm.

Two further results fit the literature. That the carry does not accumulate with repeated reading resembles the levelling off of dynamic evaluation within a few thousand characters of in-distribution text \citep{krause2018dynamic}. And the far larger gain from placing the preceding text in attention matches the finding that full attention retrieves much better than test-time training \citep{tandon2025endtoend}, and the result for Generative Adapter, a weight update built in a forward pass, which reaches an F1 of 40.2 on multi-session conversations against 66.0 for prompting with the full history \citep{chen2024generative}.

\section{Implications}

Within the scope of these perplexity measurements, a carried fast weight is worth keeping across documents of the same source, most of all related ones, and should be reset when the source changes, while within a document attention remains far stronger. For research on persistent test-time learning, the results suggest that the write of a carried state has to be trained for a later read, and that this can work even when a read-time clip discards the state's magnitude. A training signal that spans document boundaries removes the harm in our probe, but at the cost of specificity, so such a signal needs a way to keep related and unrelated text apart, for example a learned reset or a gate that sees both documents.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec-limits}

\paragraph{Capacity confound.} The TTT models train about 6.7 times as many parameters as the control. Most of their lead over the control is this extra capacity, and a control with matched parameter count, such as the same architecture trained with fast-weight updates disabled, would separate the two cleanly. It was not run for budget reasons. The within-document and between-slice gains are the measures that isolate the mechanism. The same confound applies to the redesign, whose training gain exceeds the control's by about 0.11 of its 0.234 lead.

\paragraph{Carry trained within documents only.} Every training session is one document, so the models learn to use a carried state between slices of a document but are never trained with a state carried from a different document. The cross-document result therefore measures whether a within-document carry transfers across documents. One probe trained on multi-document sessions is reported (Section~\ref{sec-mixed}), with one seed, one mixing ratio and sessions of five documents, so what such training learns in general is not established. The clip analysis in Section~\ref{ch-why} applies to both cases, since the clip saturates on every measurement regardless of how the state was built.

\paragraph{Scale.} One model of 0.6 billion parameters is studied, against the 0.6 to 4 billion range planned in the proposal, with one seed per configuration except the carry design, which has two, and the probe trained across documents, which has one, and at most 600 of 1050 optimiser steps. The null result of the naive carry is stable across 200, 400 and 600 steps, which argues against undertraining within this budget. The redesigned carry's cross-document effect is not stable, falling from $+0.010$ at step 200 to $-0.020$ at step 600, so its value at the end of the schedule is unknown. Larger models and longer training are untested. Forgetting under sequential fine-tuning has been found to grow with model size \citep{li2024revisiting}, so the balance between transfer and interference may shift at larger scale.

\paragraph{Evaluation set.} The held-out set has 106 documents, with only 6 from Book. For the original models the cross-document test rests on 24 documents, about four per source, so it can rule out only effects larger than about 0.01. The redesign's boundary suite uses 100.

\paragraph{No-clip probes.} The runs without the clip are short, 100 to 200 steps, and single-seed. They show that removing the clip does not rescue persistence early in training, not what a long unclipped run would converge to.

\paragraph{Carry design.} The redesigned carry is replicated with two seeds, which is enough to show its results are not a chance result but not to report a spread, and it exists for the Hebbian rule only. Its ablation stops at step 200 with one seed, while the relatedness results come from step 600, so which changes the relatedness effects depend on is not tested. Its relatedness gradient is measured on Wikipedia only, with relatedness defined by title mentions, which includes word overlap, so the results do not separate relatedness in topic from shared words. The context comparison gives attention one previous slice, while the carry holds a decayed summary of all earlier slices, and the dynamic-evaluation baseline of Appendix~\ref{app-dyneval} uses a sparse form on the LoRA weights of a different model, the LoRA-only control. The in-context condition of Section~\ref{sec-confirmatory} reads the prime and the target as one input of 2048 tokens, so it also changes the position of the scored tokens, and its heavy-tailed gains were examined only after the preregistered analysis. The gap between the boundary and relatedness suites remains unexplained, since accumulation over a chain of Wikipedia articles does not reproduce it.

\paragraph{Development and confirmatory sets.} The carry design, the gate bias of $-6$ and the choice of suites were all settled while looking at the same 106 SlimPajama documents and 200 Wikipedia cases. The relatedness results, the generic, in-context and chain conditions, and the pooled gains of the main evaluation are replicated on the preregistered confirmatory set (Section~\ref{sec-confirmatory}). The boundary, repeat, context and per-source results rest on the development set only. The confirmatory SlimPajama holdout has 73 documents from five sources and none from Book, because the reserved pool of long documents is small, so per-source results of the main evaluation could not be checked. The confirmatory Wikipedia cases were checked against the Wikipedia part of the training data by shared ten-word sequences, which removed one case, but a check on the decoded first 700 tokens of each training document cannot rule out every overlap. The confirmatory set tests the same model checkpoints, so it guards against selecting results on the development cases but not against design choices that suit Wikipedia in general.

\paragraph{Perplexity only.} All evidence is perplexity-based, and perplexity alone does not show how generated text or downstream behaviour changes \citep{meister2021beyond}. Lower loss can coexist with no behavioural recall \citep{song2026beyond}, and a written fact can leave a raised log-probability after the model stops using it \citep{oneill2026continually}, so our results support claims about stream and domain adaptation, not about memory. A diagnostic that withholds the earlier text, in the spirit of LongBench \citep{bai2023longbench}, would help separate what is carried from what the base model already knows. The RULER probes planned in the proposal were not run, and no behavioural test of retained information was attempted, consistent with the proposal's scope. Whether the carry helps where attention cannot reach, for example after the previous document has left the context, is not tested.

\chapter{CONCLUSION}
\label{ch-conclusion}

This report asked whether the fast weights that test-time training builds while reading one document can be carried into the next. In perplexity they can, if the carry is designed and trained for it. A gated carry trained only within documents transfers across documents according to their relatedness. Its benefit falls steadily from a related article to an unrelated one of the same source and turns to harm for text from another source, and this holds across two training seeds and on a confirmatory test set analysed by a plan fixed in advance. Most of the benefit is a generic effect of the source, and a small part is specific to the document. Training the carry across documents in a single-seed probe removes the harm but also that specific part. Repeated reading adds nothing beyond the second reading, and next to attention on the preceding text the carry recovers only about four percent of the gain.

The naive carry, by contrast, contributes nothing measurable. The norm clip that keeps the fast weights stable is saturated on every measurement, so a carried state reaches the output only as a direction, and in short probes removing the clip made the model either collapse or switch the fast weights off. Giving the carry its own zero-initialised path and training it through the boundary between slices, two changes that in a single-seed ablation appear to work together, makes that direction useful. It raises the Hebbian carry between slices from at most 0.014 to about 0.1, with the clip still saturated. Along the way the attribution shows that most of a TTT model's lead over a matched baseline is already present with its fast weights switched off, which points to its extra trainable capacity, and that within a document the delta rule adapts 1.7 to 9 times more than the Hebbian rule. The open questions are whether the gradient holds to the end of training, beyond Wikipedia and at larger scale, why a long chain harms in one suite and helps in another, and how a model can learn when to apply its carry without losing what makes it specific to a document.

\section{Future research}

For the carry design, the most direct extensions are further seeds to report a spread, the relatedness gradient on other domains and with measures of relatedness that separate topic from word overlap, a mechanism that decides when to apply the carry, and the same design for the delta rule. The most important missing checks are dynamic evaluation of the carry model itself with its carry switched off, training to the end of the schedule, further seeds and mixing ratios for training across documents, and a way to keep the document-specific part of the transfer when the carry is trained across boundaries. More broadly, the extensions are a parameter-matched control, dynamic evaluation on all weights, a carried state with a designed bound such as a normalised or gated memory, a learned rather than fixed decay of the carried state \citep{ravi2017optimization,yang2024gla}, training through document boundaries so that the carry receives a learning signal, and replication at larger scale with several seeds.

\section{Recommendations}

Three recommendations follow from the results. A carried fast weight should be kept across documents of the same source, most of all related ones, and reset when the source changes. The write of a carried state should be trained for a later read, as the redesigned carry is, rather than left to the within-document objective. And a carry trained across document boundaries needs a way to keep related and unrelated text apart, such as a learned reset, since the probe trained without one lost its advantage on related text.

\section{Dynamic evaluation baseline}
\label{app-dyneval}

This baseline is reported for completeness and supports no claim in the main text. It runs on the LoRA-only model rather than on the carry model, so the two conditions also differ in their slow weights. It adapts the LoRA weights with Adam and no decay, unlike the method of \citet{krause2018dynamic}, and it was tuned on development cases.

The LoRA-only model at step 600 takes Adam steps on its 10.1 million LoRA parameters over each prime, reads the target, and is restored exactly before the next case, with updates made on 32-bit copies. Step size and number of steps were tuned on 40 further Wikipedia cases, disjoint from the 200 reported, separately for the same-document and the related-article gain, over step sizes from $3 \times 10^{-6}$ to $10^{-4}$ and 3, 10 or 30 steps. The best settings lie on a ridge of roughly constant step size times number of steps, and settings beyond it diverge. Tuned for a related article the best setting is $3 \times 10^{-5}$ with 3 steps, and tuned for the same document $3 \times 10^{-5}$ with 10 steps. A coarser first search with plain gradient descent did not bracket its optimum.

Tuned for a related article, dynamic evaluation lowers perplexity by 0.224 on the same document and by 0.029 after a related article, and raises it by 0.019 after an unrelated article and by 0.022 after another source. Tuned for the same document, it lowers perplexity on the document's own text by 0.271 but raises it by 0.236 after a related article and by 0.284 after an unrelated one. Table~\ref{tab-dyneval} gives the paired difference from the carry, case by case.
